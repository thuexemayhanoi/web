The Grant Cycle And The Machine

The research month has a budget shape of its own, the grant arriving in tranches, the fieldwork window eating its heaviest week early, the writing month spending almost nothing, and the machine’s cost moving with the shape rather than against it, which is why the plan is written against the actual project calendar rather than against the calendar’s abstract squares. The honest ledger asks the month what it intends to do before assigning the machine its lines.

The cycle also decides the rental’s own duration questions, a project with three fieldwork months and two writing months may want the machine monthly through the first three and daily through the fourth, a blend the desk can discuss and the budget can hold, and the researcher who plans the blend in advance pays for wheels exactly when the wheels are working and not a week more.

The mistake is budgeting a flat month for a project that has none, the machine rented for the writing weeks because it was rented for the field weeks, when the calendar prints the whole story in advance. Write the plan against the project cycle. The cycle is the budget.

The Buffer, Small And Sacred

The buffer’s size is a desk conversation rather than a guess, because the division of responsibility between renter and shop is a stated-terms question, some items belonging to the shop’s service arrangements, some to the renter’s own account, and the researcher who hears the terms before signing sizes the buffer to the actual agreement, while the researcher who assumed sizes it to a rumour, and the two sizes are never the same.

The mistake is budgeting the buffer to zero and calling it efficiency, the month then borrowing from the coffee line to pay for the Tuesday, and the project’s clean ledger absorbing small dishonestities until the reconciliation finds them.

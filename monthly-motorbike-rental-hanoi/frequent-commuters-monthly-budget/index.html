The fixed layer is one rental rate, verified and published, and the living layer is written by repetition, the fuel line proportional to the route’s honest distance, the parking line proportional to the work end’s honest arrangement, and the gear line nearly flat once the first week’s purchases are made. The heavy rider who spends one evening counting the month rides the whole month inside the count, which is the calmest way a working month can be ridden.

The countable month also changes what the budget is for, because the commuter’s money buys transport rather than experience, and the budget’s honesty is measured in cost per commute, the monthly rate divided by the rides it bought, a number that makes the motorcycle the cheapest moving thing in the city and the budget’s whole case in one line. The tourist’s budget asks whether the month was worth it, the commuter’s asks only whether it ran, and a counted month always runs.

The mistake is believing a repeated month needs no planning because it repeats, when repetition is exactly what makes planning cheap and the lack of it expensive, the unplanned commuter’s month discovering its lines one at a time like everyone else’s. Count the month once, in advance, and the repetition works for the budget instead of around it.

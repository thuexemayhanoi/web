How to Rent a Motorbike in Hanoi

Renting a motorbike in Hanoi takes only a few minutes once you know the process. This step-by-step guide walks you through choosing the right bike, checking verified prices, arranging pickup at our Long Bien shop and starting your ride with confidence.

It is part of our Hanoi motorbike rental guide hub, which also covers pricing, scooters, 50cc bikes, rental areas and motorbike trips out of the city.

Semi-automatic bikes such as the Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius are the classic Hanoi choice: simple, tough and the cheapest category to rent. Automatic scooters such as the Honda Vision, Honda Click, Honda Air Blade and Yamaha Mio are even easier in stop-and-go traffic because there is no gear shifting to think about.

If you want the lightest machine, a 50cc motorbike rental keeps things simple for city errands and short rides. We also rent electric motorbikes for quiet, low-emission travel around town. For a closer look at the automatic models, see the Hanoi scooter rental hub.

Step 2: Check prices and pick a rental period

Rates depend on the bike and the rental period. Semi-automatic models rent for 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week or 900,000–1,200,000 VND per month. Automatic scooters such as the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade rent from 200,000 VND per day, and 50cc motorbikes rent for 200,000 VND per day.

Weekly and monthly rates are not simple multiples of the daily rate, so compare periods before deciding. Staying longer? Our monthly motorbike rental in Hanoi hub explains long-term hire, and the full Hanoi motorbike rental cost page lists every verified price.

Step 3: Contact us and arrange pickup

Call 0942 467 674, email nguyentuantu8x@gmail.com, or stop by the shop. We are at 112 Nguyen Van Cu, Long Bien, Hanoi, open 09:00–21:00 daily. Contact us ahead of your dates so we can confirm that the model you want is available.

When you arrive, the rental is completed at the shop. Ask anything you are unsure about — how the bike starts, where the fuel cap sits, how the lock works — before you set off.

Step 4: Inspect the bike before you ride

Before accepting the motorbike, walk around it and check the basics: front and rear brakes, lights, horn, tire condition and fuel level. Take a few photos of the bike from all sides — this protects everyone and makes the return smooth.

Test the mirrors, the kickstand and the throttle response. Our motorbike guides hub collects the everyday checks and riding basics worth knowing before you join Hanoi traffic.

Step 5: Ride safely and return on time

Wear a helmet, ride defensively and expect traffic to flow around you constantly — slow and steady is the local way. For longer rides out of the city, our Hanoi motorbike trips hub lists directions worth considering, and the renting a motorbike in Vietnam guide covers the wider legal context of riding in the country.

Agree on the return time and the expected fuel level with us when you pick the bike up, then enjoy the city on two wheels.

Before you contact a rental shop

Prepare three pieces of information first: your dates, the type of bike you prefer and how you expect to use it. A renter who needs a bike for two city days has different priorities from someone staying for a month or planning longer rides. Clear dates also make an availability answer much more useful.

If you do not know the model name, describe the category instead. You can say automatic scooter, semi-automatic, 50cc or electric motorbike. Our main Hanoi rental guide explains those categories without requiring you to choose a specific unit before you arrive.

What the website can confirm before pickup

The website can confirm the published business details and verified prices. It cannot honestly display live inventory because bikes are rented and returned throughout the day. It also should not invent a deposit, delivery promise or document rule when that information has not been confirmed for your situation.

That is why the final step before travelling to the shop is direct confirmation. For quick answers about the address, opening hours, published rates and the difference between verified information and details that need confirmation, use the FAQ.

How to compare rental periods

Choose the period that matches the real stay. Weekly rates should be compared with weekly rates, not with seven isolated daily bookings. Monthly rates should be compared with monthly rates. Our verified table shows why: Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius are 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000–1,200,000 VND per month.

Automatic scooters also vary by model. Honda Vision is 200,000 VND per day and 1,000,000 VND per week, while Yamaha Mio and Honda Click are 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week. Use the price hub rather than estimating from memory.

How to choose between automatic and semi-automatic

An automatic scooter is convenient for riders who do not want to manage gears in stop-start traffic. A semi-automatic such as Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius may suit someone already comfortable with that style and who wants the lower published daily and weekly rates. Neither category is automatically right for everyone.

At pickup, comfort matters more than a specification sheet. Check whether you can balance the available bike easily, reach the controls and see clearly in the mirrors. If you prefer automatic models, the scooter hub compares the verified options.

Documents, deposits and legal questions

Do not assume that one internet checklist applies to every renter. Document, deposit and licensing questions can depend on the bike and the rider's situation. This site therefore asks you to confirm those details directly instead of publishing a universal promise that may not apply.

Licensing and road-law questions should also be checked against current official Vietnamese information. Our Vietnam rental guide explains the planning issues but does not replace legal advice or an official source.

Your first ten minutes with the rental

Once the handover is complete, spend a few minutes getting used to the exact bike before joining heavier traffic. Check brake feel, throttle response and mirror position again. Learn how the stand works and where the indicators, horn and lights are. Small familiarisation steps reduce distraction later.

For practical riding, parking, fuel and routine checks, continue to the Motorbike Guides hub. The purpose of the rental process is not speed; it is to leave the shop knowing what you are riding, what you agreed and who to contact if you have a question.

A compact rental checklist

  • Choose the bike category that matches your riding confidence and route.
  • Check the verified rate for the same rental period you actually need.
  • Send your dates and preferred model or category to confirm availability.
  • Confirm any document, deposit or special arrangement that applies to your situation.
  • At pickup, inspect brakes, tyres, lights, horn and mirrors.
  • Take photos of the bike and learn the controls before leaving.
  • Keep the shop contact details available during the rental.

This checklist is deliberately short because the important part is not paperwork for its own sake. The goal is to make sure the bike, price, dates and expectations are all clear before you ride away.

What not to assume from a website

Do not assume that a listed bike is in stock, that a particular deposit applies to everyone, that hotel delivery is included or that a legal requirement is the same for every rider. Static pages are good at publishing stable information; they are not a substitute for date-specific confirmation or official legal sources.

When in doubt, ask one direct question rather than building a plan around an assumption. The FAQ is designed to show which answers are stable enough to publish and which should still be confirmed directly.

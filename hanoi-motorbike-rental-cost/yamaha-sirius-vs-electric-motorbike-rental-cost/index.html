Yamaha Sirius vs Electric Motorbike: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

Most rental comparisons choose between machines, but this one chooses between centuries. The Yamaha Sirius is a petrol semi-automatic underbone from the entry tier at 150,000 VND per day, and the electric motorbike is a battery-powered step-up machine at 200,000 VND per day, with verified gaps running through every rental period. The two powertrains also spend money differently once you ride away.

Below you will find the verified rental prices, what the higher tier's premium pays for, how each bike handles Hanoi traffic, and the running costs, where petrol and electric really differ. It builds on the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, sits within the Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and shares practical ground with the rental FAQ. Full numbers first, philosophy second.

This comparison crosses a line the others do not: the powertrain itself. The Yamaha Sirius runs on petrol and a semi-automatic gearbox, a mechanical formula refined over decades of Vietnamese daily riding. The electric motorbike replaces fuel and gears with a battery and a throttle, and it costs more than the Sirius on the verified price list.

Because the two machines sit in different tiers, this is a comparison with a real price attached, and a real trade attached to the price. Paying more buys a different kind of machine entirely, with different habits, different sounds, and different daily rituals. The fair way to compare them is to look at both prices first, then ask what each type of engine does for the rider.

The sections below put the verified daily, weekly, and monthly rates side by side, explain why the electric machine is priced above the entry tier, describe what riding each one feels like in Hanoi, and weigh the running costs, where the petrol-electric difference is felt most. The conclusion is a personal one, but it should be a personal one made with full numbers.

The Verified Price Cards

The daily rates open with a familiar shape. The Sirius rents at a verified 150,000 VND per day, and the electric motorbike rents at 200,000 VND per day. The 50,000 VND daily gap is the smallest meaningful unit on the price list, yet it repeats every day of your rental and sets the tone for every period that follows.

Across a week the same gap compounds into a premium worth naming. The verified Sirius week costs 700,000 VND, and the verified electric week costs 1,000,000 VND, which means a weekly renter pays 300,000 VND for the switch away from petrol. That premium either buys a riding experience you value or it does not, and a week is long enough to find out.

The month completes the picture with two different pricing philosophies. The Sirius month sits in a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, open to your quote and the rental conditions, while the electric month is a flat verified 1,500,000 VND with no band at all. Long-stay renters therefore compare a flexible low band against a fixed higher number, a choice with real structure to it, mapped across the fleet in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

What the Premium Pays For

The premium buys a different contract with the machine rather than a faster one. Electric step-up machines in this fleet are priced for their convenience, their quiet operation, and their freedom from fuel stops, and the flat monthly rate shows the fleet treating them as a serious long-stay option rather than a novelty. The tier gap is the price of that package.

For the Sirius side, the entry tier is priced the way it has always been priced: as the affordable, repairable, universal machine of Vietnamese streets. Petrol underbones carry the advantage of an infrastructure that has grown around them, from fuel stations on every corner to mechanics who know the format by ear. The lower verified rates reflect how completely that ecosystem has been solved.

Neither philosophy is wrong, which is why both tiers exist on the same list. The electric machine bets on simplicity of operation and predictability of cost, while the Sirius bets on price and familiarity. What the comparison owes you is clarity about both bets, and the running-cost section below is where the electric bet either pays off or does not.

Riding the Sirius on Petrol

On the Sirius, riding is a physical conversation. The foot-operated semi-automatic gearbox makes you choose gears, the petrol engine responds with revs and pull, and the rider stays involved in the kind of mechanical riding that has been normal in Vietnamese cities for generations. Riders who enjoy that loop often refuse to trade it away at any price.

The petrol side also brings infrastructure the electric side cannot match yet. Fuel stations are everywhere in and around Hanoi, refuelling takes minutes, and range anxiety is simply not part of the underbone experience. A Sirius rider plans routes by destination alone, never by where the next charge might come from, which is a freedom that only becomes visible when you rent a machine that needs a socket.

What the Sirius asks in return is involvement and a little noise. Shifting through dense traffic takes attention until it becomes habit, and the engine announces itself at every intersection. Riders who see that as character will love the bike and the price; riders who see it as workload should read the next section before choosing, because the electric tier exists partly for them.

Riding the Electric Motorbike

The electric motorbike does away with almost all of the routine tasks the Sirius involves. There are no gears to shift, no engine noise to read, and no fuel stops to schedule. Riding it is a quiet, single-speed, twist-and-go experience that puts the rider in the city without adding mechanical homework, which is precisely the convenience its tier is priced around.

The quiet changes the ride in ways riders notice immediately. Intersections feel calmer, mornings feel earlier and more polite, and the machine slips through residential lanes without announcing itself. In a city where noise is part of the texture, an electric ride is a different texture entirely, and for many short-stay visitors that difference is the most memorable part of the rental.

The trade is planning. An electric machine lives on its battery, and riding far from your charging arrangements means thinking about the return trip in a way petrol riders never do. Within the city and nearby neighbourhoods range is rarely an issue, but trips outside the built-up area need careful planning. The machine rewards riders whose plans stay close to home, and it taxes riders whose plans do not.

Fuel Versus Charging Costs

Where the electric tier starts earning its premium is the pump. A petrol machine needs fuel throughout the rental, and those small, frequent purchases add up over a week or a month. An electric machine replaces the pump with the socket, and for most riders the cost of the electricity a rental consumes is far below the cost of the petrol it would otherwise burn.

That advantage comes with a caveat about arrangements rather than physics. Ask at booking how charging works, where you can plug in and what the shop provides, because the price list does not say. Ask where and how the machine charges, what the shop supplies, and what happens if the battery runs flat during your rental, before any of it becomes an adventure.

For short city stays, the fuel-versus-charging difference is usually small money either way, and the choice rests on riding style. For a month, the numbers matter more: the Sirius costs 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND in rent plus a month of fuel, while the electric machine costs a flat 1,500,000 VND and almost nothing in fuel. Common rental questions beyond powertrain live in the rental FAQ.

Matching the Powertrain to Your Stay

Let the shape of your stay decide. Stays built around the Old Quarter, the lakes, and nearby neighbourhoods suit the electric machine beautifully, because charging is a non-issue when your world is compact, and the quiet ride fits the dense city. If your plan is mostly short hops in the busiest districts, the step-up premium buys genuine daily comfort.

Stays that wander suit the Sirius. Petrol infrastructure follows you across the city and far beyond it, the monthly band keeps long stays affordable, and a rider who shifts confidently gets a complete machine for the lowest verified rate on the board. The underbone also forgives ambitious plans, because the question of where to charge next simply never comes up.

Long-stay renters should work out both totals carefully. The Sirius month lands somewhere between 900,000 and 1,200,000 VND depending on the quote, the electric month is fixed at 1,500,000 VND, and fuel on one side offsets charging on the other. The rental overview at Hanoi motorbike rental explains how periods and conditions work, and both powertrains fit inside it cleanly.

Booking and Charging Questions

Both machines are available through Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu at 112 Nguyen Van Cu in Long Bien, Hanoi, and this pairing needs slightly more thoughtful booking than most, because charging arrangements matter as much as availability. Ask your dates for both models, and for the electric machine, ask the charging questions at the same time so nothing surprises you later.

Confirm the rates at booking as well. Daily and weekly figures for both machines are fixed on the verified list, the electric month is flat at 1,500,000 VND, and the Sirius month lands inside its band, so ask where your quote sits and what it includes. For electric rentals, also ask what charging equipment comes with the bike and what the return arrangements are, since those answers are shop policy rather than published rates.

In summary: the Sirius is the verified price leader at 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and a monthly band from 900,000 VND, with petrol costs and total freedom of route. The electric motorbike is the verified upgrade at 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week, and a flat 1,500,000 VND month, with quiet, simple riding and nearly free running. Two philosophies, one list, and a choice your trip will make obvious.

Frequently asked questions about Sirius vs electric motorbike rental costs in Hanoi

Which is cheaper to rent, the Sirius or the electric motorbike?

The Sirius, at every verified period: 150,000 VND against 200,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND against 1,000,000 VND per week, and a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND against the flat 1,500,000 VND electric month.

Is charging cheaper than buying petrol for a rental?

Typically yes for the amounts a rental consumes, since the electricity a small machine uses costs far less than weeks of tank refills. Exact charging arrangements and any equipment are set by the shop, so confirm what is provided when you book.

How do the monthly rates compare?

The Sirius rents within a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, while the electric motorbike carries a flat verified 1,500,000 VND month. One is flexible and potentially cheaper, the other fixed and predictable, so confirm both quotes before deciding.

Which machine is easier for a beginner to ride?

The electric motorbike. It is fully automatic and quiet, with no gears, clutch, or engine noise to manage. The Sirius uses foot-shifted semi-automatic gearing, which most riders learn within a day or two but which does add early workload in traffic.

Can I ride the electric motorbike outside the city?

Nothing forbids it, but planning becomes the constraint, because a battery needs charging arrangements the way a petrol bike needs a fuel station, and stations are far more common than sockets on the road. Keep longer routes realistic when you plan them, and ask the shop about range when booking.

Which should I choose for a month-long stay?

Weigh rent against running costs. The Sirius band tops out at 1,200,000 VND plus a month of fuel, while the electric month is fixed at 1,500,000 VND with minimal charging costs. City-bound riders often break even or better on the electric side; wandering riders usually prefer the Sirius.

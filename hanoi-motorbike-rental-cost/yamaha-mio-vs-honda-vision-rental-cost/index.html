Yamaha Mio vs Honda Vision: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

The Yamaha Mio and the Honda Vision share a format and differ by tier, which makes this the cleanest price comparison in the fleet. Both are fully automatic scooters, equally rideable by a complete beginner, so no skill dimension separates them. What separates them is the verified price list: the Mio anchors the entry tier and the Vision opens the step-up tier, and the gap runs through every period.

The verified rates are set side by side, with what the step-up tier adds when format is equal, a fair description of each machine, and the booking questions worth asking. It builds on the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, the main Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and the rental FAQ. The numbers first, the philosophy second.

Most rental comparisons hide a second question inside the first, because a price gap usually also means a format gap, geared against automatic, small against large. Not here. The Yamaha Mio and the Honda Vision are both fully automatic scooters, both twist-and-go, both equally manageable by a beginner. The only difference this comparison asks you to pay for is the tier of machine itself.

That purity makes the decision unusually clean. There is no riding-skill dimension, no gear lever to weigh, no learning curve to factor. You are choosing between the entry tier of the verified price list and the step-up tier, and the verified numbers state the price of moving between them at every rental period. What remains is the question of value.

The daily, weekly and monthly rates for both machines are set out, along with what the step-up tier offers over the entry tier when format is equal, a description of each machine, and booking advice for a comparison where the talk at the shop is short: both bikes are automatic, so the only topics are price and finish.

The Verified Prices Compared

The daily rates place the two machines 50,000 VND apart. The Mio rents at a verified 150,000 VND per day, and the Vision rents at a verified 200,000 VND per day. Since both deliver the same automatic format, that daily difference is the pure, per-day price of the step-up tier, repeated for every day of the rental.

The weekly rates scale the gap without changing it. A Mio week is verified at 700,000 VND, and a Vision week is verified at 1,000,000 VND, a difference of 300,000 VND. Spread over seven days it is the same 50,000 VND per day, now visible as a sum: the step-up week costs what two extra entry-tier days would cost, which is the clearest way to feel the gap.

The monthly figures widen the spread further. The Mio month sits in a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, and the Vision month sits in a verified band of 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND, so a long-stay renter pays roughly double for the higher tier. The full fleet structure behind these numbers is mapped in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

What the Tier Gap Buys

Because the format is identical, the tier gap buys finish rather than capability. The Vision belongs to the step-up tier, whose machines carry more comfort, more equipment, and a more polished riding experience than the entry tier, and its verified prices reflect that positioning throughout. Nothing about the Mio fails at its job; the Vision simply does the job in a nicer suit.

The step-up premium is therefore a comfort purchase in its purest form. There is no skill barrier you are paying to avoid, as there would be in a semi-automatic comparison, and no capability you cannot access at the lower price. You are paying for a more refined ride, and the test is whether that is worth 50,000 VND a day to you personally.

It is worth being blunt about the alternative use of that money, because small gaps hide in plain sight. Across a week, the 300,000 VND difference is several good meals; across a month at band midpoints, it approaches or exceeds the entire entry-tier rental cost again. The step-up tier is worth every dong for riders who value it and a quiet extravagance for riders who do not, and both answers are correct.

The Mio at Entry Money

The Mio is the entry tier automatic at its most straightforward. It is light, low, and friendly, with a compact scale that suits dense districts and smaller riders, and its automatic delivery makes it fully usable from the first minute. At 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week, it delivers complete city mobility at the lowest verified automatic rates on the list.

Its monthly band makes it the budget platform for long stays. A verified range of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month means a rider can base a month in Hanoi on the entry tier and spend the difference on the month itself. For renters whose priority is value, the Mio answers before the comparison even starts.

What the Mio does not pretend to offer is polish. It is a simple machine priced as one, and its virtues are the entry-tier virtues: lightness, economy, and the disappearance of every barrier between a beginner and the road. Riders who want comfort above all should keep reading, because the next section prices exactly that.

The Vision at Step-Up Money

The Vision is the comfort statement of the step-up tier. As a machine it leans toward easy, practical refinement, with a riding position and equipment level aimed at daily commuters who want the pleasant version of every ride. Honda position in Vietnam makes it one of the most familiar premium scooters on the street, which adds a quiet confidence to the experience.

In use, the refinements translate into a calmer city ride. The step-up tier absorbs the small frictions of Hanoi traffic, the constant stops, the tight maneuvers, with a bit more composure at each one, and riders who spend all day on the bike notice the difference more than riders making short hops. The automatic format both machines share means nothing extra is asked of the rider in return.

The Vision month tells the long-stay story plainly. At a verified 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month, it costs roughly double the Mio band, and that premium is the price of a month spent in the more comfortable seat. For a long stay built around daily riding, many renters find that worth it; for a month of occasional errands, the entry tier covers the same tasks for half the money.

Running Costs Stay Level

Running costs decline to separate two machines of the same format. Both engines are small and frugal, consumption in city riding falls in a similar range, and parking is charged per bike across the city, so the daily spend beyond the rental rate looks much the same whether the badge says Mio or Vision. Fuel is bought by the litre and burned by the kilometre, not by the tier, and neither machine carries an appetite that punishes its rider.

Ask the shop about the fuel arrangement for either machine, including how the tank is handed over and returned. The paperwork questions that come with any rental, deposits, documents, and conditions, are shared ground as well, and the answers live in the rental FAQ rather than differing by tier or brand.

The practical budget for either choice is therefore simple: the rental rate, plus fuel, plus ordinary city parking, with nothing tier-specific in between. The entire financial difference between these two machines lives on the rental line of the budget, which at least makes it easy to see and easy to decide on.

Choosing Tier Over Format

Choosing here means choosing how much comfort your trip uses. Short stays with light schedules tilt toward the Mio, because a few days of occasional hops rarely let the step-up polish express itself, and the entry rates keep the budget free for the trip. The Mio covers the same streets, the same parking spots, and the same legal rights to the road at two thirds of the daily cost.

Stays built around daily riding tilt toward the Vision, and long stays most of all. When the bike is ridden every day for weeks, the more comfortable seat, the calmer delivery, and the extra composure in traffic stop being luxuries and start being the daily experience of the rental, and the verified premium becomes a per-day purchase of ease. Commuters and month-long residents are the natural Vision renters.

The neutral way to frame it is cost-per-comfort: the Mio is the value answer at 150,000 VND per day and a monthly band from 900,000 VND, and the Vision is the comfort answer at 200,000 VND per day and a band from 1,800,000 VND. Both are automatic, both are good choices, and the rental overview at Hanoi motorbike rental explains the rental periods both use.

Same format, different tiers, and a decision that comes down to how much comfort a day of riding is worth to you.

Frequently asked questions about Mio vs Vision rental costs in Hanoi

Which is cheaper to rent, the Mio or the Vision?

The Mio, at every verified period: 150,000 VND against 200,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND against 1,000,000 VND per week, and a monthly band starting at 900,000 VND against the Vision band starting at 1,800,000 VND.

Are both machines automatic?

Yes, both are fully automatic twist-and-go scooters, so riding skill is not a factor in this comparison at all. The difference between them is tier, the entry-level Mio against the step-up Vision, not format.

Is the Vision worth the extra 50,000 VND per day?

It depends on how much you ride. The Vision offers a more refined, comfortable machine in the step-up tier. For daily, long-duration riding many renters find it worth the premium; for occasional short hops the Mio covers the same city for less.

How do the monthly rates compare?

The Mio rents inside a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, and the Vision inside a band of 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month. Long-stay renters should confirm where their quote sits within the relevant band before deciding.

Do running costs differ between the two?

Not meaningfully. Both are economical city machines with similar consumption, and parking is charged per bike. The entire financial difference between them sits in the rental rate itself, which makes the comparison unusually clean.

Which should I book for a month in Hanoi?

Let your daily mileage decide. A month of daily commuting suits the Vision comfort at its verified band, while lighter schedules suit the Mio at roughly half the monthly cost. Confirm current quotes for both and match the tier to your real usage.

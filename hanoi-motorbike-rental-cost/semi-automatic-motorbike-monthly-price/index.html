Semi-Automatic Motorbike Monthly Rental Price in Hanoi: Cost Planning Guide

Renting a semi-automatic motorbike by the month is one of the most economical transport decisions you can make during a long stay in Hanoi. The Honda Wave and the Yamaha Sirius, the two semi-automatic models available at Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu on 112 Nguyen Van Cu, Long Bien, are light and durable underbone machines built exactly for the daily riding this city demands. This guide lays out the verified monthly prices for both bikes, compares those figures against the daily and weekly rates, and shows you how to budget a full month of riding without guessing at a single number.

What a Monthly Semi-Automatic Rental Includes

A monthly semi-automatic motorbike rental in Hanoi is built around one straightforward idea: you pay a single agreed rate and keep the same machine for the whole month. For residents, language students, remote workers, and long-stay travellers, this arrangement removes the need to renew a contract every few days and lets the bike simply become part of your daily routine. The semi-automatic underbone design, with a foot-operated gear lever and no clutch lever to squeeze, remains the most practical configuration for stop-and-go riding on Hanoi's crowded streets.

Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius are the two semi-automatic models in this rental category at Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu, located at 112 Nguyen Van Cu, Long Bien, Hanoi. Both are light, narrow, and easy to park, which matters when your month involves commuting every single day. Renting by the month also signals that you intend to use the bike as genuine daily transport rather than a short holiday toy, and the shop prices that commitment accordingly.

Before you commit, it helps to read the general pricing overview on our Hanoi motorbike rental cost page, because monthly figures only make sense when you place them next to the daily and weekly rates for the same machines. A month-long booking is a bigger commitment than a weekend hire, so planning the budget in advance is worth the small effort it takes, and the sections below give you every number you need to do that planning properly.

Verified Monthly Prices for Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius

The verified monthly rate for the Honda Wave and the Yamaha Sirius at this shop sits in a band between 900,000 and 1,200,000 VND per month. The shop quotes within this range rather than publishing a single fixed figure, because the final number depends on the specific model you choose and the conditions of your rental agreement. Always confirm the exact monthly price for your chosen bike when you contact the shop, and treat only that confirmed figure as the price you will actually pay.

It is worth repeating the pricing rule that protects you as a renter: never accept an estimated price that is not verified. The figures above come directly from the shop's published pricing, and no other monthly numbers for these two semi-automatic models should be treated as reliable. If you see a different monthly figure quoted anywhere else, whether online or in conversation, treat it as unverified and ask the shop directly instead of building your budget around it.

For context, the same two models rent for 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week. Seeing the three periods side by side shows how the pricing structure rewards longer commitments, and the sections below walk through that comparison step by step so you can judge for yourself whether a monthly plan fits the length of your stay in the city.

Why Monthly Rates Save Money Over Daily Pricing

The arithmetic of daily versus monthly pricing is easy to check for yourself. Paying the daily rate of 150,000 VND for thirty days would total 4,500,000 VND if you simply renewed a daily contract every morning. The verified monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND sits far below that figure, which is why long-stay visitors almost always switch to the monthly option once their plans in Hanoi pass the two-week mark and the savings become impossible to ignore.

Even measured against the weekly rate of 700,000 VND, the monthly band wins comfortably. Four consecutive weekly renewals add up to 2,800,000 VND, which is more than double the upper end of the monthly band. The gap exists for a practical reason: a single monthly contract involves less administration for the shop, less paperwork for you, and no gaps in the rental period that need to be renegotiated when the month gets busy.

The saving only becomes real if you actually keep the bike for the full month, so honest planning matters. If your stay might end after ten days, one or two weekly rentals may fit better than a monthly contract you abandon early, and it is better to know that before you sign. Review your calendar first, then browse the main Hanoi motorbike rental page if you want to see the full range of rental periods and models in one place before you decide.

Comparing Weekly Stacking Against One Monthly Rate

Some renters try to hedge by stacking weekly contracts, paying 700,000 VND at a time and deciding week by week whether to continue. Across a full month this approach costs roughly 2,800,000 VND, and in exchange it gives you a flexibility that a monthly agreement does not. The trade-off is simple to state: stacked weeks buy the freedom to stop early, while a single monthly rate buys a much lower total cost for exactly the same machine and the same period of riding.

A useful way to plan is to treat the monthly band as a ceiling for your transport budget. If your total budget for four weeks of riding is 1,200,000 VND or more, the monthly rental fits inside it with room to spare for fuel and parking. If your budget is tighter, or your departure date is genuinely uncertain, stacking weeks keeps the exit open at the cost of a higher price per month, and that is a legitimate choice when your schedule is not your own.

Whichever route you lean toward, put both sets of numbers next to the daily and weekly figures on our Hanoi motorbike rental cost overview so the comparison stays grounded in verified prices rather than estimates. Budget decisions made from the published figures are decisions the shop can honour when you arrive to sign, and that alignment between planning and paperwork is exactly what makes monthly renting low-stress.

How Automatic Scooter Monthly Prices Compare

Automatic scooters sit in a higher monthly bracket than semi-automatic underbones. The Honda Vision is verified at between 1,800,000 and 2,000,000 VND per month, while the Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike are both listed at 1,500,000 VND per month. Against the semi-automatic band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, the difference across a month can run to several hundred thousand dong and in some pairings close to a full million, which is real money for a long-stay resident.

The premium buys a different riding experience: a twist-and-go throttle with no gear lever to operate, a storage box under the seat, and a seating position that some riders find more comfortable in dense stop-and-go traffic. Riders who have never used a foot shifter often prefer a scooter despite the higher monthly cost, while riders who value low running costs and simple mechanical layout usually stay with the semi-automatic category and keep the difference in their pocket.

If you are genuinely undecided, the price gap alone is a reasonable tiebreaker. A month on a Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius tops out at 1,200,000 VND, while a month on a Honda Vision can reach 2,000,000 VND, and that difference funds a great deal of fuel. Test your comfort with a gear lever before you commit, because the monthly rate applies whether you end up loving the bike or merely tolerating it for thirty days.

Extra Monthly Costs to Budget For

The monthly rental rate covers the bike itself, not the cost of riding it every day. Fuel is the most obvious running expense, and a semi-automatic underbone is among the cheapest machines to run in the city, with modest consumption on the short, slow trips that make up a typical Hanoi day. Build a realistic monthly fuel allowance based on how far you expect to ride each week, and treat it as a fixed part of your total transport budget rather than an afterthought.

Parking is the second regular cost. Paid motorbike parking around the Old Quarter, office districts, and apartment blocks is inexpensive by international standards, but a month of daily parking fees adds up, and a monthly parking arrangement where you live or work may be worth asking about. Also confirm before signing whether your agreement includes anything beyond the bike itself, such as helmets, because only the shop can tell you exactly what accompanies your specific monthly rental.

Wear items are the third category. A month of daily riding means tyres, brake pads, and chain tension all deserve occasional attention, and the shop can tell you which maintenance items are covered during your rental and which remain your responsibility as the rider. Get those answers in writing or at least in a saved message thread, and read the rental FAQ page first so you arrive knowing exactly which questions are worth asking at the counter.

Deposit and Paperwork Planning for Long Stays

A monthly rental usually involves the same documents as a shorter hire, but the stakes feel higher because you hold the machine for a full month rather than a few days. Expect to leave identification or a deposit arrangement as agreed with the shop, and confirm the exact deposit amount and the conditions for its return when you sign. The deposit exists to protect the bike across a long period of daily use, so understanding the return inspection in advance avoids surprises on your final day.

Long-stay renters should also photograph the bike at pickup, noting any existing scratches or marks, and keep those photos until the deposit is back in your hands. A month is long enough for small cosmetic changes to accumulate from everyday parking, and a documented starting condition makes the return conversation straightforward for both sides. This habit costs nothing, takes two minutes, and settles most disputes before they can even begin.

Paperwork planning also means understanding the rules you will ride under for a full month, from helmet requirements to the local traffic customs that shape daily life in Hanoi. The rental FAQ page answers the most common questions renters raise about documents, deposits, and responsibilities, and reading it before you commit to a monthly contract takes only a few minutes. Arriving with those answers already in hand makes the sign-up conversation shorter and clearer for everyone involved.

How to Plan Your Rental Period and Payments

Start your planning with the calendar rather than the price list. Count the days you genuinely expect to need the bike, add a small buffer for schedule changes, and compare that number against the breakpoints where weekly and monthly pricing cross. For stays of a month or longer, the verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND is almost always the right choice for a Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius, and the longer you stay the more decisive that gap becomes.

Next, agree the payment rhythm with the shop. Some monthly renters prefer to settle the full month upfront, while others ask about arrangements suited to longer stays, and only the shop can confirm what applies to your specific booking. Ask about renewal terms as well, because a month from now your plans may have changed, and knowing in advance how to extend or finish the rental keeps you in control of both the bike and the budget.

Finally, tie the whole plan together in a single budget line: the monthly rate for the bike, your fuel allowance, expected parking costs, and a small reserve for the unexpected. A rider who budgets 1,200,000 VND for the rental itself has a realistic ceiling for the Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius, and everything beyond that is running cost rather than rental cost. With the numbers verified in advance, a monthly semi-automatic rental in Hanoi becomes one of the simplest transport decisions a long-stay visitor can make.

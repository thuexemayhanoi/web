Yamaha Mio vs Honda Air Blade: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

The Yamaha Mio and the Honda Air Blade are both automatic, which strips every riding-skill argument out of this comparison and leaves the pure question of money against machine. The Mio anchors the entry tier at 150,000 VND per day; the Air Blade carries the sportier end of the step-up tier at 200,000 VND per day, with a flat 1,500,000 VND month that makes the long-stay arithmetic unusually interesting.

This guide sets the verified rates side by side, prices the step-up premium at every period, hears both machines out, and works the monthly sums in full, because this pairing hides the smallest step-up gap in the fleet. It builds on the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, the main Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and the rental FAQ for practical questions.

The Yamaha Mio and the Honda Air Blade answer the same question with different enthusiasm. Both are fully automatic scooters, so no skill difference divides them, but the Mio represents the entry tier of the verified price list, where economy is the point, and the Air Blade represents the sportier end of the step-up tier, where the machine is the point. The price list prices that difference of intent.

This comparison therefore runs on a single axis: money against machine. There is no gear lever to consider, no format to weigh, and no learning curve to cost into the decision, which leaves the verified rate gap as the entire story of the choice. Every rental period states the premium plainly, and the rider decides whether the sportier machine earns it.

Below, the verified daily, weekly, and monthly rates for both models are set side by side, the premium is priced at each period, each machine makes its case, and the monthly arithmetic, where the Air Blade flat rate changes the shape of the decision, is worked through in full. The goal is a decision you can defend with numbers.

Verified Prices, Side by Side

The daily rates differ by a clean third. The Mio rents at a verified 150,000 VND per day, and the Air Blade rents at a verified 200,000 VND per day, so every day of the rental carries a 50,000 VND premium for the step-up machine. On a single day it is pocket change; on a fortnight it is a dinner for two.

The weekly rates keep the same shape. Seven days on the Mio are verified at 700,000 VND, and seven days on the Air Blade are verified at 1,000,000 VND, a weekly premium of 300,000 VND. Anyone comparing a week of each should notice that the premium equals two full days of entry-tier riding, which is a useful measure of what the upgrade costs in opportunity.

The monthly rates deserve special attention, because here the two machines are priced in different currencies of certainty. The Mio month is a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, flexible and open to your quote. The Air Blade month is a flat, verified 1,500,000 VND, fixed regardless of conditions. The fleet structure behind both is laid out in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

Pricing the Step-Up Premium

The premium buys the step-up tier at its more assertive end. The Air Blade carries a sportier road presence than the comfort-first machines of its tier, and its pricing reflects a machine aimed at riders who enjoy the riding itself, not merely the arrival. The entry tier asks nothing extra and delivers everything necessary; the step-up tier adds character and finish on top.

Notably, the premium here is smaller than the step-up premium elsewhere in the fleet. Against the Air Blade flat 1,500,000 VND month, the Mio band tops out at 1,200,000 VND, a gap of 300,000 VND at most, where other step-up comparisons open wider. The Air Blade is, in monthly terms, the most affordable step-up machine in the fleet, which makes this pairing the closest entry-versus-step-up decision on the list.

That closeness is worth respecting rather than dismissing. When the monthly gap is only a few hundred thousand dong, the question stops being whether the upgrade is affordable, which it now plausibly is, and becomes whether it is wanted. Renters who assumed the step-up tier was out of reach should check this pairing before deciding, because the arithmetic may surprise them.

The Mio Case for Economy

The Mio case is the fleet case for economy. At 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week, it delivers complete automatic mobility at the lowest verified rates for the format, and its monthly band from 900,000 VND makes it the cheapest platform for a long stay. For riders whose bike is a tool, the Mio is the correct tool at the correct price.

It is also a genuinely good machine rather than a compromise. Light, low, and manageable, it suits the dense centre of Hanoi beautifully, and its automatic delivery keeps every ride simple regardless of traffic. The entry tier does not mean entry quality; it means the price of the job, and the Mio does the job completely and reliably.

What the Mio does not offer is theater. It is quiet about itself, and riders who want their machine to add something to the experience, presence, finish, a bit of sport in the step-off, will notice its absence on the Air Blade side of the ledger. For many renters that absence is invisible, and for them the Mio wins with room to spare.

The Air Blade Case for Upgrading

The Air Blade case is the case for spending a little on the experience. As the sportier face of the step-up tier, it brings a firmer, more confident feel and a machine that rewards being ridden rather than merely operated. Riders who enjoy the mechanical side of the street, without wanting gears to manage, are exactly who it is priced for.

Its flat monthly rate is a quiet second argument. Where other machines quote inside bands, the Air Blade month is fixed at a verified 1,500,000 VND, which gives long-stay renters a number that cannot move. Budgeting a month in Hanoi around a fixed rental line is simpler than budgeting around a range, and some renters value that certainty as much as the machine itself.

The automatic format it shares with the Mio means none of this comes at the cost of effort. The Air Blade asks nothing mechanical of its rider; it simply responds with more substance when asked. The premium, in other words, buys a richer version of the same easy experience, which is a coherent thing to want for a month of daily riding.

The Monthly Arithmetic That Matters

The monthly arithmetic deserves its own section because it is where this comparison is closest. A Mio month lands somewhere between 900,000 and 1,200,000 VND depending on the quote, and an Air Blade month is fixed at 1,500,000 VND. The monthly premium therefore ranges from 300,000 to 600,000 VND, spread across thirty days of riding.

Converted to daily terms, the premium is between 10,000 and 20,000 VND per day, the price of a small drink or less. That is the honest scale of this decision for long-stay renters: not whether the upgrade is affordable, but whether a few thousand dong a day is a fair price for a month on the sportier machine. Riders who will use the bike daily will likely say yes without much deliberation.

For shorter stays the premium is proportionally larger, at the full 50,000 VND per day, so the decision sharpens as the stay shortens. A three-day rental pays 150,000 VND extra for the step-up experience; a month pays as little as 300,000 VND total. The same upgrade is cheap by the month and noticeable by the day, which is unusual and worth knowing before you choose a rental length.

Choosing by Schedule, Not by Style

Schedule, not style, should make the final call. A short visit with light riding duties points to the Mio: the premium is proportionally highest, the stays are too brief for the step-up character to express itself, and the entry rates keep the budget for better things. Light users rarely regret the cheaper machine, and the Mio is a pleasant one to ride.

A long stay with daily riding points to the Air Blade. When the bike carries you every day for a month, the sportier, more substantial machine becomes part of the month itself, and a premium of 10,000 to 20,000 VND per day prices that experience almost embarrassingly low. Commuters and month-long residents are the natural Air Blade renters, and the flat 1,500,000 VND month is built for exactly that decision.

The neutral summary is short. The Mio is the economy answer, verified at 150,000 VND daily, 700,000 VND weekly, and a monthly band from 900,000 VND. The Air Blade is the experience answer, verified at 200,000 VND daily, 1,000,000 VND weekly, and a flat 1,500,000 VND month. Both are automatic, both are honest, and the overview of Hanoi motorbike rental explains the period structures behind both.

Frequently asked questions about Mio vs Air Blade rental costs in Hanoi

Which is cheaper to rent in Hanoi, the Mio or the Air Blade?

The Mio, at every verified period: 150,000 VND against 200,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND against 1,000,000 VND per week, and a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND against the Air Blade flat 1,500,000 VND month.

Are both machines automatic?

Yes. Both the Mio and the Air Blade are fully automatic, twist-and-go scooters, so no skill difference separates them. The comparison is purely about tier and price, not about riding format or learning curve.

How much more does the Air Blade cost over a month?

Between 300,000 and 600,000 VND, depending on where the Mio quote lands in its 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND band against the flat 1,500,000 VND Air Blade month, which is the smallest step-up monthly gap in the fleet.

Is the Air Blade worth the extra cost?

For daily, long-duration riders, usually yes: the premium can work out to 10,000 to 20,000 VND per day on a month. For occasional short hops, the Mio covers the same city at the lower verified rates and the premium is less justified.

Do the two differ on fuel or running costs?

Not meaningfully. Both are economical city machines with similar consumption, and parking is charged per bike. The financial difference between them lives almost entirely in the rental rate itself.

Which should I choose for a month-long stay?

Compare two numbers with the shop: your Mio monthly quote within its band, and the flat 1,500,000 VND Air Blade month. Daily riders often take the Air Blade for the sportier experience; lighter users usually prefer the Mio savings.

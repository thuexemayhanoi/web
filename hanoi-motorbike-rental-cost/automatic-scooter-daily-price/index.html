Automatic Scooter Daily Rental Price in Hanoi: Cost Planning Guide

An automatic scooter rents in Hanoi at one of two verified daily prices: 150,000 VND per day for the entry tier and 200,000 VND per day for the step-up tier. This guide plans the daily rental around both numbers, because the honest question for an automatic rider is not just what a day costs, but which tier the day actually needs.

We cover the two verified daily rates, which machines sit in each tier, what each tier buys, day-by-day budget planning, the break-even math against the published weekly rates, and the deposit, gear and fuel questions that turn a headline rate into a real budget. The Two Verified Daily Rates

An automatic scooter in Hanoi rents at 150,000 VND per day or 200,000 VND per day, and both figures come straight from the shop's published price list. Nothing in this guide pads them with invented extras, promotions or bundle discounts. The two numbers are the entire daily story for twist-and-go machines, and every decision below follows from choosing between them.

The entry tier at 150,000 VND per day holds the Yamaha Mio and Honda Click, light city automatics, and sits alongside the semi-automatic Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius at the same price. The step-up tier at 200,000 VND per day holds the Honda Vision, the Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike, plus the 50cc scooter in its specialized small-engine class.

A daily rental is also the most flexible structure. Nothing is committed beyond the day, machines can be swapped between days, and a plan that might shrink carries no penalty. Flexibility is what the premium over the weekly rate's daily average actually buys, and for genuinely short stays it is worth paying.

The Entry-Tier Automatics

The Yamaha Mio and Honda Click rent at a verified 150,000 VND per day, and they are the cheapest twist-and-go machines in the fleet. What the rate buys is the honest city automatic: light, low, easy to park, and simple enough that the first minute of riding is the whole learning curve. For a plan built around the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem Lake and market runs, the entry tier is not a compromise, it is the right-sized tool.

The entry tier also publishes the longer structures: 700,000 VND per week and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, the monthly confirmed at the counter as a range. That fuller ladder matters even on a daily plan, because it defines what the daily rate is for, the short stays, and what it is not, the long ones.

The entry-tier mistake is treating it as a lesser machine. The Mio and Click are automatics by design, not automatics by compromise, and riders whose day is city-only lose nothing but 50,000 VND by skipping the step-up tier. The tier question is a day-shape question, not a status question.

The Step-Up Tier Automatics

The Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade and electric motorbike rent at a verified 200,000 VND per day, alongside the 50cc scooter in its own specialized class. The tier buys different machines for different days: the Vision is the storage and comfort choice, the Air Blade is the performance choice, and the electric swaps fuel stops for charging.

The step-up tier publishes 1,000,000 VND per week, with monthly figures differing by model: the Vision at 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month, and the Air Blade and electric at 1,500,000 VND per month. The 50cc publishes only the daily rate, with longer 50cc stays quoted by the desk rather than estimated anywhere, including here.

The step-up mistake is paying for a tier the day does not use. A plan of short lanes and lake loops needs neither the Vision's under-seat storage nor the Air Blade's acceleration, and 50,000 VND per day adds up across a stay. The tier earns its premium only when the day actually calls for what it buys.

Planning a Daily Budget

Plan the day from three numbers: the rental, 150,000 or 200,000 VND depending on tier, a small fuel line, and the minor costs of riding, paid parking at a few thousand VND per stop where lots are used. The rental is fixed; the other two lines stay modest on city automatics, which is what makes a daily budget honest rather than aspirational.

Plan the routes around the day's real shape. A typical first day is the Old Quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake, where a light automatic makes the narrow lanes effortless. A second day stretches to the Temple of Literature and West Lake, where the step-up tier's comfort earns its keep on longer runs. A third crosses to Long Bien for the bridge views and market. Each of those days fits an automatic, and none needs more than the tier that matches it.

Build the budget for the plan you have, not the plan you might have. If the day is one loop and an evening, the daily rate plus fuel is the whole story. If the day might become five days, read the break-even section before paying for day two, because that is where the arithmetic changes.

The budgeting mistake is paying for structure you do not use. Riders book a week for a two-day plan and let five unused days evaporate, or ride a month on daily rates. Both errors dwarf the 50,000 VND that separates the tiers, so get the structure right before fine-tuning the machine.

The Break-Even Math

The entry tier crosses over at five days. Five daily rates total 750,000 VND against a 700,000 VND week, so the week is already cheaper at five days and saves 350,000 VND over a full seven paid daily. Any entry-tier stay touching five days should ask for the weekly conversion at the counter.

The step-up tier crosses over at exactly five days. Five daily rates total 1,000,000 VND, matching the weekly rate to the dong, and every day after the fifth is money the week would have kept. Below five days the daily rate stays honest on both tiers: two days cost 300,000 or 400,000 VND, three cost 450,000 or 600,000 VND, four cost 600,000 or 800,000 VND, all under the respective weeks.

The monthly rates reward longer stays on both tiers. The entry tier's 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND month and the step-up tier's 1,500,000 to 2,000,000 VND months mean a stay stretching toward ten days should be a monthly conversation, not a series of daily ones. Automatics Versus Semi-Automatics

The fleet also rents semi-automatic underbones, the Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius at 150,000 VND per day, with a foot shifter and no clutch lever. The honest difference is control versus simplicity: a semi-automatic rewards riders who like shifting and offers a more traditional ride, while a twist-and-go automatic removes the entire gear question.

For most visitors the automatic is the pragmatic choice, because Hanoi's stop-start traffic makes constant shifting a chore and constant simplicity a relief. Riders with motorbike experience may prefer the semi-automatic's engagement, and at the same 150,000 VND daily rate the choice is free.

The comparison mistake is assuming automatic means automatic-only skills. A twist-and-go still needs two hands, balance and traffic sense, and the brief handover conversation matters on both kinds of machine. Choose the transmission that matches the rider, not the reputation, and the daily rate on either side of that choice is the same honest 150,000 VND.

Deposits, Gear and Extras

The daily rate is the headline, not the whole transaction. Ask what the deposit is, in what form it is held, and what has to be true at return for it to come back in full. Cash deposits get receipts and card holds get release dates, and both questions take ten seconds each at the counter.

Ask what is included with the day: helmets for each rider, a lock, rain ponchos. Inclusion is a question, not an assumption, and confirming it at handover prevents the curb-side surprise of a missing second helmet with a passenger already waiting. Hanoi rain arrives fast, and a poncho fits under the seat without ceremony.

Fuel is the daily extra on the petrol machines, confirmed at handover, full out and full back is the common pattern, and budget a small top-up for city distances. The electric motorbike swaps that line for a charging conversation, which is one question at handover and near-zero cost on a city day. The total daily cost of riding an automatic sits low in any Hanoi budget, whichever tier the machine comes from.

The extras mistake is discovering terms after signing. Read the one-page agreement at the counter, in the shade, before riding. Every verbal answer about deposit, fuel and inclusions becomes a promise on that page, and the two minutes of reading are the cheapest part of the day. Choosing the Right Daily Rate

The honest ladder is short. One to four days: pay the daily rate, 150,000 VND in the entry tier or 200,000 VND in the step-up tier, and enjoy the flexibility. Five to nine days: take the week, 700,000 VND entry or 1,000,000 VND step-up, both cheaper than five or more daily rates. Ten days and beyond: ask about the month, 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND entry or 1,500,000 to 2,000,000 VND step-up, and stop counting days.

Choose the tier by the day's shape. City-only plans fit the entry tier's Yamaha Mio or Honda Click at 150,000 VND. Days that need storage, comfort or performance fit the step-up tier at 200,000 VND. The 50,000 VND difference is real money across a stay, but it is small money next to a structure error, so fix the structure first and the tier second.

Yamaha Sirius vs Yamaha Mio: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

Comparing rental costs usually means hunting for the cheaper option, but the Yamaha Sirius and Yamaha Mio make that hunt unnecessary. Both models appear on the same verified price list in Hanoi with identical rates for daily, weekly, and monthly rental. That turns this comparison into something more useful: a clear look at what each format delivers for exactly the same money.

This guide compares the two models across price, riding experience, and running costs, so you can decide which one earns your rental days. It builds on the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, fits within the main Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and answers practical questions from the rental FAQ. Since the Sirius and the Mio are priced identically, the real decision is about which bike fits you.

The Yamaha Sirius and the Yamaha Mio sit side by side in the same rental fleet, and they represent two different answers to one question: how do you want your engine to deliver its power? The Sirius is a semi-automatic underbone, which means you shift gears with your left foot but never touch a clutch lever. The Mio is a fully automatic scooter that handles every ratio change for you, quietly and without asking.

What makes this pairing unusual for a cost comparison is that the verified price list treats both models exactly the same. Renting a Sirius costs the same as renting a Mio at every rental period on the board, from a single day to a full month. When the price column cannot separate the two bikes, the meaningful comparison moves elsewhere, into how each format feels on the street and what it costs to live with day after day.

This guide walks through the verified rental prices for both models, explains why the shop prices them identically, compares the riding experience of a semi-automatic underbone against an automatic scooter, and looks at fuel and daily running costs. The goal is simple: by the final section you should know which format fits your trip, confident that your wallet will not notice the difference either way.

Verified Rental Prices for Both Models

Start with the headline figure. Both the Sirius and the Mio rent for 150,000 VND per day, verified on the current price list. That daily rate applies whether you point at the underbone or the scooter, so a weekend visitor choosing between them is choosing between riding styles, not between price tags. For short stays the decision carries no financial weight at all, which is rarer in a comparison than you might think.

Stretch the rental to a week and the numbers stay locked together. Both models cost 700,000 VND per week, which works out noticeably cheaper per day than paying the daily rate and rewards the longer commitment equally on each side. A seven-day rental of either bike saves real money compared with paying day by day, and the saving is identical whichever model you ride out of the shop on the first morning.

Monthly rental follows the same pattern with one twist. Both the Sirius and the Mio sit in a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, and the exact point within that band depends on the conditions of your rental. Long-term renters should confirm the current monthly figure with the shop directly, since that is where the only real cost question in this comparison lives. Why the Cost Is Identical

Identical pricing is not an accident of rounding. Rental fleets typically price by tier rather than by individual model, grouping bikes that occupy the same market position and serve the same kind of rider. The Sirius and the Mio are both small-displacement Yamaha machines aimed at everyday city use, so they land in the same tier together, and the price list rewards that logic with one clean rate for both.

There is also a practical logic at work. When two models rent at the same rate, the shop can manage availability flexibly, and renters can choose on genuine preference rather than being nudged toward whichever bike happens to be cheaper that week. For you, that means the choice between a Sirius and a Mio stays a pure riding decision, unclouded by a price gap that might otherwise push you toward the wrong format for your skills.

It also simplifies planning. If your budget is built around the verified daily, weekly, and monthly rates for this tier, you can lock in that budget before deciding which model to take, and even change your mind at the counter without redoing the maths. Identical pricing turns what looks like a cost comparison into a preference comparison, which is a far better problem to have when you are standing at the rental desk.

Riding the Yamaha Sirius in Hanoi

The Sirius rides like a classic Vietnamese underbone. You shift through the gears with your left foot, and although there is no clutch lever to manage, you still decide when the engine changes ratio. That gives the bike a connected, mechanical feel that many experienced riders enjoy, because it keeps you engaged with what the engine is doing at every moment on the road.

In Hanoi traffic, that engagement cuts both ways. Moving off from a red light wants a downshift or two, and holding a smooth flow in stop-and-start streets means working the gear lever regularly. Riders who grew up on geared bikes find the Sirius intuitive and rewarding from the first hour. Complete beginners sometimes find the extra input demanding in the opening days, before the shifting pattern settles into muscle memory.

The underbone layout also shapes comfort and posture. The Sirius carries its classic step-through frame, sits taller than most scooters, and leans toward a more traditional riding position that many riders find natural on longer rides. For anyone who wants a bike that feels like the machines locals ride every day, the Sirius delivers an authentic slice of Vietnamese street life at exactly the same rental cost as its automatic sibling in the same row.

Riding the Yamaha Mio in Hanoi

The Mio takes the opposite approach to the same job. Its fully automatic transmission means there is nothing to shift and nothing to coordinate: you twist the throttle and the engine manages its own ratios. For anyone who has never operated a geared bike, this removes the single biggest learning hurdle in riding, which is a large part of why automatic scooters dominate rental counters across Southeast Asia.

In dense city traffic, that simplicity earns its keep. Hanoi streets ask for constant speed adjustments, tight low-speed maneuvers, and frequent stops, and an automatic absorbs all of that without asking for gear changes. Riding the Mio through the Old Quarter at rush hour becomes mostly a matter of throttle, brakes, and awareness, which frees your attention for the traffic unfolding around you rather than for your left foot.

The trade-off is feel. An automatic insulates you from the engine, so the mechanical connection that Sirius riders enjoy is simply absent. Some riders consider that a fair price for convenience, and others find it remote, but with rental costs identical you can let pure preference decide. Beginners, nervous riders, and anyone planning dense urban riding lean toward the Mio for exactly these reasons, and nobody pays a premium or saves a dong by following that instinct.

Fuel Economy and Daily Running Costs

Fuel is the running cost most likely to differ between two models, but in this pairing the difference is negligible. Both the Sirius and the Mio use small engines tuned for city riding, and their real-world consumption in Hanoi traffic falls in the same frugal neighbourhood. Neither bike will meaningfully out-spend the other across a normal rental period, so fuel should not tilt your choice in either direction.

How much you actually spend at the pump depends far more on your habits than on your model. Daily distance, throttle discipline, and whether you ride in the busiest hours all move the needle more than the badge on the bike. Fuel arrangements also vary between rentals, so confirm at pickup whether you return the bike with a full tank or settle for what you used, and budget for fuel as a shared cost rather than a model-specific one.

Beyond fuel, the daily running costs are broadly shared. Parking in central Hanoi typically costs a few thousand dong per stop, secure overnight parking may add a little more, and both machines fit the same parking spots with equal ease. The overall cost of a riding week in Hanoi looks almost the same whichever Yamaha you choose, which is precisely what the identical price list implies. Practical questions about deposits and extras are covered in the rental FAQ.

Matching the Bike to Your Trip

Match the format to your trip, not to a price difference that does not exist. Short tourist stays concentrated around the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem, and the lakes favour the Mio, because automatic delivery keeps dense traffic manageable and lets you focus on navigation rather than gear selection. If your itinerary is mostly short hops between neighbourhoods, the scooter simplicity is worth more than any specification on paper.

Longer stays and more varied routes give the Sirius room to shine. Riders commuting on a daily schedule, carrying a pillion passenger, or heading onto bigger roads often appreciate the control of choosing gears, and the underbone platform feels planted at open-road speeds. Experienced riders who already know how to shift will find the Sirius more engaging, and they pay nothing extra for that engagement, which is the whole point of this comparison.

For month-long rentals the format question matters more than the rate, because you will live with the bike every day. Think honestly about your route, your comfort with shifting in traffic, and how many riders will share the machine. Frequently asked questions about Sirius vs Mio rental costs in Hanoi

Is the Yamaha Sirius cheaper to rent than the Yamaha Mio?

No. Both models carry the same verified rates: 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND. The price list treats them as equals, so your budget plays no role at all in choosing between the two bikes.

Which model is easier for a first-time rider?

The Mio is the easier start. Its automatic transmission removes gear shifting entirely, so you twist the throttle and go. The Sirius asks you to work a foot shifter and read engine revs, which is manageable but takes a few rides to feel natural in Hanoi traffic.

How much does a month of Sirius or Mio rental cost?

Both models sit in the same verified monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND. Where your quote lands inside that band depends on the rental period and conditions, so confirm the exact figure with the shop before you commit to a long stay.

Do fuel costs differ between the Sirius and the Mio?

Not in any meaningful way. Both engines are small and frugal around town, and real-world consumption in Hanoi traffic is close enough that fuel spend rarely decides the comparison. Your riding style, trip distance, and fuel arrangements matter far more than the model choice.

Can I switch from the Sirius to the Mio during my rental?

That depends on the shop arrangements rather than the price list, since both models rent at identical rates. If you think you might want to swap formats mid-rental, raise it when you book so the shop can tell you what is possible and on what terms.

Which model should I choose for riding around the Old Quarter?

Most short-stay visitors pick the Mio, because automatic gearing is one less thing to manage in dense Old Quarter traffic. The Sirius suits riders comfortable with a gear lever who prefer the feel of a traditional underbone. Cost is identical either way.

Yamaha Sirius vs Honda Vision: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

Some rental pairings cost the same and some cost differently, and this one belongs to the second group. The Yamaha Sirius anchors the entry tier at 150,000 VND per day, while the Honda Vision opens the step-up tier at 200,000 VND per day, and the verified gap runs through every rental period on the list. Comparing them is therefore a real budget exercise.

This guide lines up the verified daily, weekly, and monthly figures for both models, explains where the price gap comes from, and weighs what each tier delivers for the money. It builds on the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, sits alongside the main Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and shares practical answers with the rental FAQ. The aim is a tier decision you can defend.

Not every comparison in a rental fleet ends in a tie. The Yamaha Sirius and the Honda Vision occupy different tiers of the same verified price list, so choosing between them changes what you pay at every rental period. The Sirius is the semi-automatic underbone anchoring the entry tier, while the Vision is the automatic scooter leading the step-up tier.

That structure makes this a genuine cost decision rather than a preference exercise. The gap is modest per day but compounds across a week and becomes serious money across a month, so the honest question is not which bike is better in the abstract, but whether the extra features of the step-up tier earn the extra dong for your specific trip.

This guide puts the two price cards side by side, explains why the fleet prices them differently, describes what each model delivers for its money, and works through the running costs that sit on top of the rental rate. By the end you should know which tier your trip actually needs, which is the only comparison that matters when the numbers differ.

Verified Rental Prices Side by Side

The daily figures set the tone. The Sirius rents at 150,000 VND per day, and the Vision rents at 200,000 VND per day, both verified on the published list. A single day of Vision costs as much as a day and a third of Sirius, which is a small gap for a short hop but the opening move of a pattern that grows with every day you keep the bike.

Over a week the difference becomes harder to ignore. Seven days on the Sirius cost 700,000 VND, while the same week on the Vision costs 1,000,000 VND, a verified gap of 300,000 VND. That is roughly two extra days of entry-tier riding paid for the step-up experience, so weekly renters should be sure the upgrade is worth it before signing.

The monthly comparison is the starkest. The Sirius sits in a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, while the Vision sits in a band of 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month. Depending on where each quote lands, a Vision month can cost close to double a Sirius month. Long-term renters should confirm both figures with the shop, and the wider price landscape is mapped in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

Where the Price Gap Comes From

The gap exists because the fleet prices by tier, and the two models belong to different ones. The entry tier holds simple, light, economical machines built around the basics of city transport, and the Sirius is its semi-automatic representative. The step-up tier holds the more feature-rich automatic machines, and the Vision opens that tier at its verified rates.

What the step-up tier buys is comfort and convenience rather than raw speed. Machines like the Vision are the everyday choice of Vietnamese commuters who want an easy, practical ride, and the rental price reflects that positioning. You are not paying for a bigger engine badge so much as for a more finished experience, and whether that finish matters is a question only your itinerary can answer.

For budget building, the practical takeaway is that the gap is predictable at every period. Daily, weekly, and monthly rates for both models are published, so you can compute the cost of stepping up before you arrive at the counter. Predictable gaps are good gaps, because they let you make the decision calmly at your desk instead of under pressure at the shop.

What the Sirius Delivers at Entry Price

At 150,000 VND per day, the Sirius is one of the cheapest complete riding packages in Hanoi. It delivers independent transport, a durable underbone platform, and a direct mechanical riding experience with foot-shifted gears and no clutch lever to manage. For riders comfortable with shifting, it covers every practical need a city stay can generate at the lowest verified daily rate on the board.

The entry price also buys simplicity of a different kind: mechanical honesty. A semi-automatic underbone has fewer systems between you and the road, and its format has been the workhorse of Vietnamese streets for decades. Parts, familiarity, and roadside respect for the format are everywhere, which is part of why it anchors the entry tier at its modest weekly and monthly figures.

The Sirius trade-off is effort. You work the gear lever through changing traffic, the underbone posture is more involved than a seated scooter, and there is no premium finish to soften the experience. Riders who treat those things as part of the fun get a bargain; riders who treat them as chores should read the next section before deciding.

What the Vision Adds at Step-Up Price

The Vision spends the extra money on ease. It is a fully automatic scooter, so there is nothing to shift and nothing to coordinate, and its layout is built around comfortable, upright, step-through riding. For newcomers to two wheels, it removes the learning curve that the Sirius assumes, and it does so with the polish of one of Vietnam most popular commuting machines.

In stop-start traffic, that polish earns its keep daily. Twist-and-go delivery makes tight maneuvers and constant pace changes almost thoughtless, and the scooter format carries everyday practicality, with bodywork and storage suited to helmets, bags, and shopping runs. For a tourist day built around short hops, or a workday built around errands, the Vision turns each ride into a low-effort task.

Whether the step-up experience is worth the step-up price depends on the rider. If your stay is short and your budget loose, the extra comfort is pleasant insurance. If you are watching every dong, the entry tier covers the same city at the same speed with the same legal rights to the road, and the premium becomes a luxury rather than a necessity.

Running Costs Beyond the Rental Rate

Beyond the rental rate, the two models cost almost the same to run. Both engines are small and frugal by design, and fuel consumption in city riding sits in a similar neighbourhood for each, so pump spending tracks your mileage far more than your model choice. Neither bike carries a running-cost penalty that would shift the comparison by itself.

Fuel arrangements are set at the counter, not by the model, so confirm whether the bike comes with a full tank and how it should be returned. Parking costs in central Hanoi are per-bike rather than per-tier, so a Sirius and a Vision pay identical parking at every stop. Insurance and paperwork questions are common to both tiers and are answered in the rental FAQ.

The one running-cost line worth flagging is time. An unfamiliar semi-automatic can cost a beginner a day or two of cautious, slower riding while gear changes become natural, whereas an automatic is fully usable from the first minute. That difference never appears on a receipt, but on a short trip it can matter as much as any verified price on the list.

Matching the Choice to Your Budget

Budget-first renters have a clear answer: the Sirius delivers the required transport at the lowest verified rate at every period, and the monthly band makes it the obvious platform for a long, cheap stay. If the goal is mobility per dong, and you are comfortable shifting gears, the entry tier wins this comparison before any softer factors are counted.

Comfort-first renters should look at the Vision the opposite way. The higher daily, weekly, and monthly rates buy the easiest possible riding experience, and for a month-long stay the gap amortizes into a daily difference small enough that many riders gladly absorb it. The format suits beginners, errand-heavy schedules, and anyone who wants riding to ask as little as possible.

The middle path is honest self-assessment. Riders who already shift confidently lose little by taking the Sirius and gain a meaningful saving; riders who do not should weigh the cost of a steeper first days against the premium for automation. A minute of confirmation removes the only uncertainty left.

The verdict is a clean split. The Yamaha Sirius is the value pick at 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and a monthly band starting at 900,000 VND. The Honda Vision is the comfort pick at 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week, and a monthly band around twice as high. Choose the tier your trip needs, confirm the current rates, and either choice rides well.

Frequently asked questions about Sirius vs Vision rental costs in Hanoi

Which is cheaper to rent in Hanoi, the Sirius or the Vision?

The Yamaha Sirius, at every verified period. It rents at 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week against the Vision at 200,000 VND and 1,000,000 VND, and its monthly band starts far lower.

How much cheaper is the Sirius over a week?

Exactly 300,000 VND, verified on the published list: 700,000 VND for the Sirius week against 1,000,000 VND for the Vision week. That gap equals two full days of entry-tier rental, so weekly renters should be deliberate about the upgrade.

Is the Honda Vision worth the extra cost?

It depends on the rider rather than the bike. The Vision offers fully automatic, low-effort riding that suits beginners and comfort-focused stays. Riders comfortable shifting gears usually get everything they need from the cheaper Sirius.

What is the monthly price difference between the two models?

The Sirius band is 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, and the Vision band is 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month, both verified. Depending on the quotes, a Vision month can approach double a Sirius month, so confirm exact figures with the shop.

Do the two models differ much on fuel?

Not meaningfully. Both are small, economical city machines, and consumption in Hanoi traffic is close enough that fuel rarely influences the tier decision. Daily distance and riding style move the fuel bill far more than the model.

Can I start on the Sirius and move up to the Vision later?

Tier changes are a matter of shop arrangements rather than the published price list, since each tier is charged at its own rates. If a mid-rental switch sounds useful, raise it when booking so the shop can confirm what is possible.

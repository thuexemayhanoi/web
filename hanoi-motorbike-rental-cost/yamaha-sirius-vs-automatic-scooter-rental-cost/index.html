Yamaha Sirius vs Automatic Scooter: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

Ask whether the Yamaha Sirius is cheaper than an automatic scooter and the honest answer is: which one? The automatic family in the verified Hanoi rental list spans two tiers, from machines that match the Sirius price exactly to machines that cost a third more per day. This guide prices the whole category instead of pretending it has one price.

Below you will find the verified daily, weekly, and monthly rates for the Sirius alongside the entry and step-up automatics, a clear statement of where prices match and where they diverge, the riding trade between gears and no gears, and booking guidance for a wide family of machines. One Bike Against a Whole Category

This is not a duel between two models but between one model and an entire family of them. On one side stands the Yamaha Sirius, the semi-automatic underbone anchoring the entry tier. On the other stands the automatic scooter category, a spread of twist-and-go machines that crosses two price tiers in the verified Hanoi rental list.

That spread matters, because a category comparison cannot quote one price honestly. Entry-tier automatics like the Yamaha Mio and Honda Click share the Sirius price point exactly, while step-up automatics like the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade sit a tier above it. The answer to which is cheaper therefore depends on which automatic you mean, and this guide prices the whole family rather than a sample.

The plan below walks through the category tier by tier, marks exactly where prices match the Sirius and where they leave it behind, compares the semi-automatic riding experience with the twist-and-go one, and ends with booking guidance for a family of machines wider than most visitors expect when they first walk up to a rental counter in Hanoi.

The Verified Rates, Tier by Tier

Begin with the verified daily figures, because they split the category cleanly. The Sirius rents at 150,000 VND per day. The entry-tier automatics, the Mio and the Click, rent at the same 150,000 VND per day. The step-up automatics, the Vision and the Air Blade, rent at 200,000 VND per day. One line of the price list, two answers to the cost question.

The weekly figures repeat the structure. A Sirius week is verified at 700,000 VND, identical to a Mio or Click week, and 300,000 VND below the 1,000,000 VND week of a Vision or Air Blade. For any rental shaped like a week, the comparison is really between formats at the entry tier and tiers at the step-up price.

Monthly rates complete the map. The Sirius month sits in a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, a band it shares with the entry automatics. The Vision month sits in its own band of 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND, and the Air Blade month is a flat 1,500,000 VND. The full structure is charted in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

When the Prices Match

Where the prices match, the comparison becomes pure riding preference. At 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and a shared monthly band, choosing between the Sirius and an entry automatic costs nothing either way. The decision reduces to a single question, whether you want to shift gears or not, and that question has no wrong answer.

The matching prices also say something about how the fleet is organised. The entry tier collects machines that do the same job, light, economical, everyday city transport, and prices the job rather than the mechanism inside it. A rider who takes the Sirius over a Mio is not buying more or less bike; they are buying a different interface with the same city.

For budget-minded renters, that symmetry is good news twice over. Whatever your period, the entry tier holds your price steady, and knowing the semi-automatic option never carries a premium means riders who prefer gears can have them free. Conversely, riders who fear gears pay nothing to avoid them, at least within this tier, and both riders walk away with the same honest bill at the same verified numbers.

When the Prices Diverge

Where the prices diverge, the step-up tier is what you are buying. Against the Sirius at 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week, the Vision and Air Blade cost 200,000 VND and 1,000,000 VND respectively, and their monthly figures stand 300,000 VND to nearly a million above the Sirius band. The premium is real at every period and compounds with time.

What it buys is the step-up experience: automatic convenience wrapped in more substantial machines, with the Vision oriented toward easy, practical comfort and the Air Blade carrying a sportier road presence, both priced accordingly. Riders who value that package find the gap fair; riders who simply want transport find the Sirius delivers the same streets at the lower verified rates.

The honest framing is that the step-up premium is a comfort purchase, not a toll for the category. Automatics as such are never more expensive than the Sirius at the entry tier, so nobody pays extra simply for wanting twist-and-go. The premium attaches to the higher tier of machine, and it can be declined without giving up automation at all.

The Riding Trade: Gears Versus None

The riding comparison sits at the centre of this pairing. The Sirius runs a foot-shifted semi-automatic gearbox, so the rider selects every ratio and hears every engine note that comes with it. Automatics of both tiers remove that entirely, delivering power through a transmission that asks nothing and tells nothing, which most riders describe as easier and some describe as less involving.

In Hanoi traffic, ease has real value. The city asks for constant speed changes, low-speed maneuvers, and patient creeping, and an automatic absorbs all of it without ceremony. The Sirius turns the same streets into a more interactive exercise, one that suits riders who enjoy the mechanics of riding and tires riders who just want to arrive without homework.

The learning curves differ sharply. An automatic is fully usable from the first minute by almost anyone, while the Sirius asks newcomers for a day or two of practice before shifting becomes instinct. On a short trip that difference can consume a noticeable share of the rental, which is a cost no price list displays but every first-time rider weighs before choosing a format.

Running Costs Across the Formats

Running costs decline to referee this fight. The Sirius and every automatic in this fleet runs a small, frugal engine, and real-world consumption in city riding lands in a similar range across the whole comparison. Fuel arrangements are set at the counter, parking is charged per bike, and no member of the automatic family carries a hidden daily cost that tilts the scales.

Where the formats differ subtly is in where they spend your attention rather than your money. Automatics spend none of it on gearing, the Sirius spends a little until habit forms, and that difference, already mentioned as learning time, is the only running cost that meaningfully separates the formats on the road. Everything else in a riding week, fuel stops, parking, and the occasional wash, looks the same whichever badge you carry.

For the paperwork side of renting, deposits, documents, and rental conditions, both formats are identical territory, and the answers live in the rental FAQ. The practical conclusion is simple: budget for the rental rate and fuel, expect nothing exotic in between, and let the format conversation happen at the riding level where it belongs.

Choosing Format and Tier Together

The clean way to choose is to answer two questions separately. First, the format question: do you want to shift gears? If the answer is no, the automatic category is yours, and if the answer is yes, the Sirius is the entry tier built for you. Second, the tier question: do you want the step-up machines or the entry ones? Only this question has a price attached, and it is the only one that moves your budget at all.

A beginner on a short city stay typically lands on an entry automatic, taking the matched price and the easiest possible ride in one decision. A confident rider on the same stay often lands on the Sirius, trading automation for engagement at no cost. A comfort-focused rider on a long stay may land on a step-up automatic, paying the premium for a month of easier riding, and the flat 1,500,000 VND Air Blade month makes that budget simple.

Long-stay renters should compare the monthly structures side by side: the shared entry band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, the flat 1,500,000 VND Air Blade month, and the 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND Vision band. The step-up automatics, the Vision and the Air Blade, cost more at every period and deliver a more finished ride for it. Frequently asked questions about Sirius vs automatic scooter rental costs in Hanoi

Is the Yamaha Sirius cheaper than an automatic scooter in Hanoi?

It depends on the tier. Entry automatics like the Yamaha Mio and Honda Click match the Sirius exactly at 150,000 VND per day, while step-up automatics like the Honda Vision and Air Blade cost 200,000 VND per day.

Which automatics cost the same as the Sirius?

The entry-tier ones: the Yamaha Mio and the Honda Click. Both share the Sirius verified rates of 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and the same monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND.

Which automatics cost more, and how much?

The step-up pair. The Honda Vision rents at 200,000 VND daily, 1,000,000 VND weekly, and 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND monthly, while the Honda Air Blade rents at 200,000 VND daily, 1,000,000 VND weekly, and a flat 1,500,000 VND month.

Is a semi-automatic like the Sirius harder to ride than an automatic?

Somewhat, especially at first. The Sirius needs foot-shifted gear changes in traffic, while automatics need none. Most riders adapt within a day or two, but beginners usually find the automatic format easier from the very first ride.

Which should a beginner choose, the Sirius or an automatic?

Most beginners are better served by an automatic, and the entry tier makes that choice free: the Mio and Click rent at exactly the Sirius rates. Nothing forces a beginner onto a geared bike to save money, which is worth knowing before booking.

How do monthly rates compare across the whole comparison?

The Sirius shares a 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND monthly band with the entry automatics, the Air Blade month is flat at 1,500,000 VND, and the Vision band runs 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND. Confirm the exact quote for your chosen machine with the shop.

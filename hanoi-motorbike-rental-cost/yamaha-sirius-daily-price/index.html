Yamaha Sirius Daily Rental Price in Hanoi: Cost Planning Guide

The Yamaha Sirius rents at a verified 150,000 VND per day in Hanoi, sitting in the entry tier alongside the Honda Wave, Yamaha Mio and Honda Click at the same rate. This guide plans the daily rental around that number: what the rate buys, what it does not, when the daily rate is the right structure, and when the weekly rate takes over.

We cover the verified price, what the Sirius is as a machine, day-by-day budget planning, the break-even against the 700,000 VND weekly rate and the 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND monthly rate, and the extras, deposit, gear, fuel, that turn a headline rate into a real budget. The Verified Daily Price

The figure is verified against the site's own price list: the Yamaha Sirius rents at 150,000 VND per day. That is the number to plan a short stay against, and the number the desk charges for a day on this machine.

The same machine has two other verified rates that matter the moment the stay lengthens: 700,000 VND per week and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, the exact monthly figure confirmed with the desk for your dates rather than estimated. This guide focuses on the daily rate and does the crossover arithmetic in full later.

What the daily rate buys is a machine for a calendar day of riding at the entry tier of the fleet. It is the rate short-stay visitors start with, because it matches how short stays work: one day of exploring, then a decision about the next.

Treat 150,000 VND as the anchor of this guide. Every comparison and budget below uses it, and the moment your honest day count changes, the anchor moves to the weekly or monthly figure instead.

What the Sirius Buys

The Yamaha Sirius is a semi-automatic underbone in the classic Vietnamese mold: light, rugged, simple to maintain, and built for exactly the lanes and boulevards it will spend its rental week on. It is one of the country's most familiar machines, which also means every mechanic on every street knows it.

In rental terms the Sirius sits in the entry tier at 150,000 VND per day alongside the Honda Wave, Yamaha Mio and Honda Click. The tier is priced for city-scale riding: commuting, errands, market runs and Old Quarter exploring, the work these machines were designed for.

Inside the tier, the Sirius and the Wave are the semi-automatic pair, the Mio and the Click the fully automatic pair, and all four cost the same per day. Riders who want to shift with a heel-toe rocker take the Sirius or the Wave; riders who want twist-and-go take the automatics. Price stays out of the decision entirely.

The honest summary for a day-rate renter: simple, light, cheap to run, and completely at home in the city it lives in. Riders planning daily river crossings with a passenger or long open stretches may prefer the step-up tier from 200,000 VND per day, and the comparisons in this hub cover that decision.

Planning a Daily Budget

A day of Sirius riding at 150,000 VND carries a few predictable side costs: a modest fuel top-up, small parking fees at watched lots, and lunch, which happens anyway. The machine is the cheapest serious line in a Hanoi day, which is why day rentals anchor so many trip budgets.

Plan by days on short stays. Two days at 150,000 VND is 300,000 VND, three days is 450,000 VND, four days is 600,000 VND. Writing the multiplication down before the counter keeps the structure conversation honest and prevents the quiet shock that undisciplined day-by-day renting produces on the last day.

Plan the routes to the machine's strengths. The Sirius is happiest on lane-scale riding, so keep the first day inside neighborhoods you can name and let the map expand as confidence grows. The rental cost stays flat while the riding grows, which is the daily rate's quiet charm.

The budgeting mistake is treating the day rate as the only number on the menu. The day rate is the entry rung of a three-rung ladder, and the next rung, 700,000 VND per week, converts any five-day plan instantly. Keep the whole ladder in view from day one.

The Break-Even Math

The arithmetic is exact. The Sirius weekly rate is 700,000 VND and the daily rate is 150,000 VND. Four days daily costs 600,000 VND and stays cheaper than the week. Five days daily costs 750,000 VND, which is 50,000 VND above the weekly rate for the identical machine.

The break-even point therefore sits between four and five days. Any stay of five days or more on the same machine should be booked weekly, and across a full seven days the weekly rate saves 350,000 VND against daily renting: 700,000 VND versus 1,050,000 VND.

The monthly math is steeper still. The Sirius rents at 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, roughly six to eight days of daily rates. Long-stay riders never multiply the day rate by thirty; they ask for the monthly figure when the stay approaches ten days, and the ladder resolves itself.

The mistake the math prevents is loyal daily renting, extending a good rental one day at a time because each day feels small. Riders who do this cross the five-day edge without noticing and pay weekly money for daily terms. Ask the desk to run your probable dates against all three structures; it takes a minute.

Sirius Versus Other Daily Rates

The fleet's daily rates split into two tiers. The entry tier, the Yamaha Sirius, Honda Wave, Yamaha Mio and Honda Click, rents at 150,000 VND per day. The step-up tier, the Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade and an electric model, rents at 200,000 VND per day. The gap is 50,000 VND per day, roughly two coffees.

The entry tier is the right daily rate for lane-scale riding, errands and Old Quarter-scale exploring. The step-up tier earns its 50,000 VND when the day includes bridge ramps, a passenger, or longer stretches at boulevard speed. The decision belongs to the day's routes, not the machine's badge.

Inside the entry tier, as noted, all four machines cost the same 150,000 VND per day, so choose by transmission style and seat comfort. The Sirius suits riders who like the semi-automatic rhythm and a machine that every street mechanic recognizes; the automatics suit riders who want one less thing to learn.

The comparison mistake is paying the step-up rate for entry-tier riding. A rider whose map never leaves the lanes pays 50,000 VND per day for capacity the day never uses. Match the tier to the day and the daily rate stays honest all week. The Hanoi motorbike rental cost hub lays every model against every period.

Deposits, Gear and Extras

The daily rate is the headline, not the whole transaction. Ask what the deposit is, in what form it is held, and what has to be true at return for it to come back in full. Cash deposits get receipts and card holds get release dates, and both questions take ten seconds each.

Ask what is included with the day rate: helmets for each rider, a lock, rain ponchos. Inclusion is a question, not an assumption, and confirming it at handover prevents the curb-side surprise of a missing second helmet with a passenger already waiting.

Fuel is the daily extra. Confirm the arrangement at handover, full out and full back is the common pattern, and budget a small daily top-up for city distances. The Sirius sips fuel, which keeps the total daily cost of riding at the bottom of any Hanoi budget.

The extras mistake is discovering terms after signing. Read the one-page agreement at the counter, in the shade, before riding. The agreement is where the verbal answers about deposit, fuel and inclusions become promises, and the two minutes of reading are the cheapest part of the day.

Questions to Ask About Day Rates

Run four questions at the counter and the day rate is settled. Is the Sirius available for my exact dates at 150,000 VND per day. What is the total on my dates, written down, not multiplied in my head. What are the deposit terms. What gear is included.

Ask the fifth question if the stay has any stretch in it: what does the week cost for the same machine. At 700,000 VND, the answer converts any five-day plan instantly, and desks expect the question daily. A good counter conversation takes three minutes and ends with the right structure for the real stay.

Ask about support too, even for a one-day rental: the number to call if the machine has a problem, and the swap procedure if a check fails. One-day rentals break less often than month-long ones, but the question costs nothing and the answer rides in your pocket.

The question mistake is haste. The day rate tempts riders into a two-minute transaction, keys first, questions later. Flip the order, questions first, keys second, and the day starts with everything already settled. The rental FAQ collects the common answers in one page.

When to Move Off the Daily Rate

The daily rate is the right choice for genuinely short stays: one to four days of city riding on a machine that fits the plan. Inside that window, 150,000 VND per day is honest, flexible and impossible to beat for the Sirius's kind of work.

Move to the weekly rate at five days. The arithmetic is absolute: five daily rates cost 750,000 VND and the week costs 700,000 VND for the identical machine. The crossover point is the moment your plans touch a fifth day, so ask for the conversion rather than extending daily.

Move to the monthly rate when the stay stretches toward ten days and beyond. At 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, the Sirius costs roughly the price of a coffee per day, and the exact figure inside the range depends on the machine and terms, so confirm it with the desk for your dates rather than estimating.

Yamaha Mio Weekly Rental Price in Hanoi: Cost Planning Guide

The Yamaha Mio rents at a verified 700,000 VND per week in Hanoi, which averages 100,000 VND per day when the week is used fully. This guide plans a weekly rental around that number: what the weekly rate buys, how it beats the daily rate, what to check at handover, and when a shorter or longer period is the better structure.

We cover the verified price, the Mio as a machine, a realistic weekly budget for a seven-day stay, the break-even against the 150,000 VND daily rate and the 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND monthly rate, and the deposit, gear and fuel questions that turn the headline rate into a real budget. The Verified Weekly Price

The verified weekly rate for the Yamaha Mio in Hanoi is 700,000 VND for a full week on the same scooter. That figure comes straight from the shop's published prices, and this guide adds nothing invented to it: no promotions, no bundles, no estimates. The number on the price list is the planning number.

Divided across seven days, the week averages 100,000 VND per day, against 150,000 VND per day at the daily rate. The saving is 350,000 VND over a full week paid daily, which is why the weekly rate is the default structure for any stay touching five days or more.

The price sits in the entry tier. The Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Click share the same verified structure, 150,000 VND per day and 700,000 VND per week, so choosing inside the tier is a choice of machine, not of price. The step-up tier, Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike, rents at 200,000 VND per day and 1,000,000 VND per week.

A weekly rental is also a commitment decision. The week books one scooter for seven days, so the plan should be honest about how many of those days the Mio will actually roll. A week with four riding days still beats four daily rates, but only barely, and that math belongs before the counter, not after.

What the Mio Buys for a Week

The Yamaha Mio is a compact automatic scooter built around ease. A week of Hanoi riding is exactly its job: twist-and-go simplicity in daily traffic, a light narrow frame that slips into the tight parking rows that pass for parking in Hanoi, and frugality that keeps a week of fuel on the small side of the budget.

The automatic transmission matters most over a week, because a week is long enough for fatigue. Stop-and-go traffic for seven days is easier on a machine with no clutch and no shift timing, and riders new to Vietnam consistently find the Mio the least demanding option in the entry tier.

For riders, the honest summary is that the Mio is easy transport, not spectacle. It carries a backpack and modest shopping, takes a passenger for short hops, and will not turn heads at a cafe. For a week of city living it is the correct answer, and the entry-tier weekly price is the cheapest verified way to live that week on two wheels.

Planning a Weekly Budget

Plan the week from three lines: the fixed 700,000 VND rental, a modest fuel line, and the small daily costs of riding, paid parking at a few thousand VND per stop where lots are used. The Mio's frugality keeps the fuel line tiny, and nothing else in a city week moves the budget.

Set the week around a realistic rhythm. A typical tourist week is Old Quarter evenings, a Hoan Kiem loop, a Temple of Literature and West Lake day, a market run, and one trip across Long Bien bridge. Each ride is short, and the Mio carries a week of them without complaint. Riders planning bigger loops should confirm the route with the desk first.

Keep a day un-ridden deliberately. A rainy Hanoi afternoon happens, and on the weekly rate a parked day costs roughly 14,000 VND of unused daily value, close enough to zero to ignore. This is the quiet advantage of the week over the day: the day rate punishes a rest day at full price, while the week absorbs it.

The budgeting mistake is planning seven full riding days. Plan five or six honest days, let weather or a walking day take the rest, and the weekly rate still wins comfortably. A budget with slack survives contact with a real Hanoi week.

The Daily Rate Comparison

The Mio's daily rate is 150,000 VND, so five daily rates total 750,000 VND against the 700,000 VND week. The break-even sits at five days: any stay of five days or longer should be booked weekly, and the counter can convert an ongoing daily rental on request. Ask, because conversion terms belong to the desk.

Below five days the daily rate stays honest. Two days cost 300,000 VND, three cost 450,000 VND, four cost 600,000 VND, all under the week. The two structures are tools for different plans, and the honest planner matches the structure to the calendar.

The comparison mistake is extending daily past the fifth day. Riders do it out of inertia, and the arithmetic quietly costs 50,000 VND per day. Mark the fifth day as decision day and the week takes over at exactly the right moment.

The Monthly Rate Comparison

The Mio's monthly rate sits in a verified range of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, and the exact figure depends on the machine and terms, so confirm it with the desk rather than estimating. Even at the top of the range, a month costs less than two weeks booked weekly, which is a dramatic saving for long stays.

The break-even between weekly and monthly is early: roughly nine to ten days of weekly rentals already approach the monthly figure. Ten daily rates would cost 1,500,000 VND, above the whole range, so stays of ten days or more should ask about the monthly rate before paying for a second week.

The honest ladder is: days below five pay daily, five to nine pays weekly, ten and beyond asks the desk for the monthly figure. The Hanoi motorbike rental cost hub puts every model against every period in one place.

Mio Versus Other Weekly Rates

Within the entry tier the weekly price is identical: Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio all rent at 700,000 VND per week. The Wave and Sirius are semi-automatic underbones with a foot shifter, while the Mio and Click are twist-and-go automatics, so the real choice inside the tier is transmission feel, not money.

The step-up tier costs 300,000 VND more per week. The Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade rent at 1,000,000 VND per week as larger automatics with more storage and stronger delivery, and the electric motorbike rents at the same weekly figure with charging instead of fuel. The premium buys machine, not status.

There is also the 50cc option for riders who want the smallest engine class, at a verified 200,000 VND per day. For weekly terms on a 50cc machine, the desk confirms the figure for your dates, which is how a verified-prices shop works.

The comparison mistake is upgrading without a reason. A week of city lanes does not need the step-up tier, and 300,000 VND buys several very good meals in Hanoi. Upgrade for storage or comfort, not for the brochure in your head.

Deposits, Gear and Extras

The weekly rate is the headline, not the whole transaction. Ask what the deposit is, in what form it is held, and what has to be true at return for it to come back in full. Cash deposits get receipts and card holds get release dates, and both questions take ten seconds each.

Ask what is included with the week: helmets for each rider, a lock, rain ponchos. A week is long enough for Hanoi rain, and included gear beats improvised gear every time. Inclusion is a question, never an assumption, and confirming at handover prevents a mid-week surprise.

Ask about support before it is needed: the number to call if the machine has a problem, and the swap procedure if a check fails during the week. A weekly rental is a relationship, and shops that rent by the week expect these questions.

The extras mistake is signing in a hurry. The weekly agreement is one page and takes two minutes to read, and every verbal answer about deposit, fuel and inclusions becomes a promise on that page. Read it in the shade before riding off, and the week starts with nothing unresolved.

When the Week Is the Wrong Rate

The week is the wrong structure for stays of four days or fewer, where the daily rate totals less than 700,000 VND, and for stays of ten days or more, where the monthly range of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND should be confirmed instead. Between those boundaries, five to nine days, the week is exactly right, and the 100,000 VND daily average is the cheapest verified way to ride the Mio in Hanoi.

It is also the wrong rate when the plan is uncertain. If the trip might shrink to three days or stretch to three weeks, say so at the counter, because a good desk would rather structure the rental correctly once than unwind it twice. Uncertainty is a planning input, not an embarrassment.

Yamaha Sirius Weekly Rental Price in Hanoi

A week is the natural length of a Hanoi holiday, and the weekly rate is the row of the price list built for it. At this operator the Yamaha Sirius rents for a verified 700,000 VND per week, a figure that undercuts seven days of daily rental by a third and covers exactly the seven days most visitors actually spend in the city. For renters planning a real week on the semi-automatic underbone, it is the best value the fleet list offers.

This guide covers the verified weekly price, the arithmetic that makes it cheaper than paying daily, what the week includes, and how to budget the riding costs around it. Every figure matches the published Hanoi motorbike rental cost hub, the machine context sits in the Hanoi motorbike rental cluster, and deposit and paperwork questions are answered in the site FAQ.

The weekly rate covers the Sirius for seven days, together with what every rate at this operator includes: the machine, a helmet, and the support of the yard across the rental. It is the same package as the daily rate, simply priced for the renter whose plans are measured in weeks rather than hours.

What the rate does not cover is everything the city adds. Petrol goes in the tank at your own cost, parking in the busier districts is charged by the session, and any traffic fine is the rider's own affair. A week of riding multiplies these running costs, which is why they get their own section below.

The weekly renter also gets what the daily renter cannot: a settled week. Time to learn the machine's habits, to find the parking that works, to plan the days that deserve riding and the ones that do not. Seven days is also long enough for the machine to stop being new: the controls settle, the mirrors stay where they are put, and the week acquires a rhythm of its own.

The verified Yamaha Sirius weekly price

The published price for a weekly Yamaha Sirius rental at this operator is 700,000 VND per week. Like the daily rate, the weekly figure is a single number on the published list, the same for every renter, and the counter confirms it at handover with the rental agreement recording it.

The single figure is what makes the weekly rate easy to plan around: no band to interpret, no conditions to uncover, one number that the calendar either justifies or does not. Put beside the other periods, the figure tells a clean story: the same machine rents at 150,000 VND per day and at a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, and the week sits between them as the middle rate most renters actually need.

The Sirius shares its weekly band with the Honda Wave, Yamaha Mio and Honda Click, so the four cheapest machines on the list are interchangeable at this price. For renters deciding between the two riding styles, a week at 700,000 VND is the cheapest full-size test either style offers. Renters comparing underbone against scooter at this figure are comparing machines, not money, which is the best kind of comparison to make.

The arithmetic: weekly against daily

Seven days of Sirius rental paid daily would cost 1,050,000 VND at 150,000 VND each. The weekly rate for the same seven days is 700,000 VND, a saving of 350,000 VND, exactly one third. That is the size of the discount a week commits to buy.

The break-even falls on the fifth day. Four days daily is 600,000 VND, still under the week; five days daily is 750,000 VND, 50,000 VND over it. Any week that includes five or more riding days is cheaper rented weekly, and the remaining two days are, in effect, free.

That cushion is the practical argument for the weekly rate: it absorbs uncertainty. A renter who plans five riding days and rides all seven has saved the most; a renter who plans seven and rides five has still saved 50,000 VND against the daily alternative. The only week the daily rate wins is the one with four or fewer riding days, and that week should have been rented daily in the first place. The Honda Wave's version of the same arithmetic is set out in the Honda Wave weekly price guide.

How the weekly price compares across the fleet

The 700,000 VND weekly band holds the four cheapest machines on the list: the Honda Wave, the Yamaha Sirius, the Yamaha Mio and the Honda Click. Two are semi-automatic underbones, two are fully automatic scooters, and all four cost the same per week. The renter's only decision at this price is which machine suits the riding.

One tier up, the 1,000,000 VND weekly band holds the Honda Vision, the Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike. The step up from the Sirius is 300,000 VND over a week, which is real money for differences that matter to some riders and not to others. The Sirius is the workhorse end of that choice: the machine that treats a week of city riding as a light workload.

Above both bands sits the monthly rate, where the Sirius shares the Wave's 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND figure. The relationship between the weekly and monthly rates is the one comparison that can change a renter's plan: two weeks of weekly rental already exceed the top of the monthly band, so the single week-or-month question is worth more than every other comparison in this guide combined. Renters whose week might become two should ask the counter about the month before paying for the second week.

Planning a week of riding costs

Fuel is the first running cost and the Sirius's friendliest one. The machine is built for economy, and a week of mixed riding uses a modest amount of petrol. Set a small daily fuel allowance, track it for the first two days, and the week's fuel budget settles itself from there. A week is also long enough to find the fuel stations that sit on the daily routes, the ones a daily renter never learns; by the third day the week has its own map.

Parking is the second cost and the one that adds up. The busier districts charge by the session, so a week that touches them daily meets the charge daily. Ask at the counter which areas charge and which street parking is free; the answer is local knowledge, and over seven days it saves more than a careless week spends.

The third cost is the fine to avoid. A week of riding is seven days of the city's traffic rules, and the rules apply to a rented Sirius exactly as to an owned one. The riders who end a week at the published price are the ones who rode it as if they were paying for their own machine, because in every sense except ownership, they were. The published rate plus a careful week is the whole cost of the rental; the published rate plus a careless one is only the floor of it.

When a week is the right period

The weekly rate fits the classic Hanoi visit: the traveller with five to seven riding days inside a seven-day stay, who wants the city and its day trips under their own power. For that renter the week is not merely cheaper than paying daily; it is the difference between a rental and a schedule. It is the period the price list was arguably designed around: seven days, one figure, one third off the daily alternative.

It also fits the practical week: the resident needing a machine for seven days while theirs is in a shop, the short work assignment, the visiting relative who wants independence for the length of a stay. Whenever the need is exactly a week, the weekly rate is the honest row of the price list to point at.

The week stops being right at both edges of its range. Under five riding days, the daily rate wins; beyond ten, the monthly band wins, and the longer-term options are gathered in the monthly motorbike rental Hanoi hub. The week between those edges belongs to the 700,000 VND figure, and it is the period this guide exists to help renters recognise.

Extending or shortening a week

A week is seven defined days, and the rental agreement names them. Returning late without an agreed extension can turn a cheap week into an expensive lesson, so mark the end date the day you sign, and think about any extension a few days before the week closes rather than the day after.

Extending is a conversation at the counter: the yard can continue the rental on the published figures, whether as a second week or as a move to the monthly band. Because the Sirius shares the Wave's prices at every period, an extension never forces a machine change either; the renter who likes the Sirius keeps it for as long as the stay lasts. The yard answers both versions of the question daily, and neither version takes longer than the ride home.

Shortening is rarer and simpler: the week was committed, the week is paid, and the unused days are the price of the certainty that bought the discount. That is why the honest week is planned before signing: count the riding days, match them to the rate, and let the calendar confirm the arithmetic.

A counter checklist for weekly renters

Six questions make a week safe. The rate, 700,000 VND, confirmed on the agreement. The dates, all seven of them, start and end. The deposit terms, amount and conditions for return, in plain words. The fuel arrangement, level in and level out.

The fifth question is the support channel: who to call if the machine needs attention in the middle of the week. The sixth is the extension question: what a longer stay costs and how to arrange it before the week closes. Six answers, five minutes, one quiet week.

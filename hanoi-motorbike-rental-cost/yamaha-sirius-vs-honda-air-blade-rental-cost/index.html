Yamaha Sirius vs Honda Air Blade: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

The Yamaha Sirius and the Honda Air Blade are separated by more than a badge. On the verified Hanoi price list they sit in different tiers, and every rental period reflects it: 150,000 VND against 200,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND against 1,000,000 VND per week, and a banded month against a flat one. This comparison works out what that money buys.

The pages below lay the two price cards side by side, explain the tier logic behind the gap, and describe what each machine delivers once the money is settled. It connects to the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, the wider Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and the rental FAQ for practical questions. Where the rates differ, the decision deserves numbers first.

The Yamaha Sirius and the Honda Air Blade share the same streets but not the same price card. The Sirius holds down the entry tier of the verified Hanoi rental list, while the Air Blade sits a tier above it, so this pairing costs different amounts of money at every rental period. Any honest comparison between them has to start with the numbers rather than the badges.

The two machines also answer different riders. The Sirius is a semi-automatic underbone, foot-shifted and mechanically direct, built around simple, dependable transport. The Air Blade is an automatic step-up machine, positioned in the fleet for riders who want a more assertive, more finished ride without giving up everyday practicality. Same streets, same city, two different contracts with the rider.

This guide compares the verified daily, weekly, and monthly rates for both models, examines why the fleet places them in different tiers, describes what each one delivers where it counts, and adds the running-cost picture on top. The goal is a simple outcome: knowing exactly what the price gap buys, and whether your trip needs it.

Verified Prices Head to Head

Day by day, the Sirius is the cheaper choice by a clear margin. Its verified rate is 150,000 VND per day, against 200,000 VND per day for the Air Blade. That 50,000 VND daily difference is small enough to ignore for a single day of riding, and large enough to matter the moment your stay extends past a long weekend.

By the week, the gap has grown into real money. Seven days on the Sirius are verified at 700,000 VND, while the same seven days on the Air Blade cost 1,000,000 VND. A weekly renter pays 300,000 VND for the step up, which is two full days of entry-tier riding, so the Air Blade week has to earn its premium through the riding experience it provides.

The month is where the comparison turns structural. The Sirius rents in a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, while the Air Blade carries a flat verified rate of 1,500,000 VND per month with no band at all. Depending on the Sirius quote, the monthly gap ranges from a few hundred thousand dong to roughly half again. The full tier structure is laid out in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

Why the Air Blade Costs More

The price gap tracks the tier system rather than any single specification. Entry-tier machines in this fleet are the light, simple, economical platforms, and the Sirius is the semi-automatic flagship of that group. Step-up machines bring more presence, more equipment, and a more premium riding experience, and the Air Blade is priced as part of that group from the first day to the thirtieth.

For the renter, the useful framing is value density. The entry tier buys complete, functional mobility, and every feature beyond that is a discretionary purchase. The Air Blade tier exists for riders whose trip benefits from the extras, and the flat monthly rate shows the fleet pricing its step-up machines with the long-stay renter in mind, a predictable number instead of a negotiating range.

Because the gap is identical in structure at every period, daily, weekly, and monthly differences can all be computed in advance. There is no scenario where the Sirius outprices the Air Blade on this list, and no scenario where the premium changes size dramatically. Predictability like that turns a rental decision into arithmetic, which is exactly what a cost comparison should produce.

Riding the Sirius at Entry Money

At its verified rates, the Sirius is the value engine of this pairing. Riders who are comfortable shifting with a foot lever get a complete, proven machine at 150,000 VND per day, a weekly figure of 700,000 VND, and a monthly band that makes it one of the most affordable long-stay platforms in the fleet. If transport per dong is the metric, the Sirius has already won.

The riding experience backs the price up. The underbone format is the default machine of Vietnamese daily life, which means every repair street, every parking attendant, and every fellow rider knows it by heart. The semi-automatic gearbox keeps the rider involved without demanding clutch skills, and the direct connection between input and response suits people who enjoy riding as an activity rather than a commute.

Its limits are the mirror of its strengths. The Sirius asks for consistent gear work in heavy traffic, offers a more basic ride than the step-up tier, and saves you money partly by leaving out the polish. For confident riders on a budget, none of that costs anything. For hesitant riders, the first days can charge a different kind of price, in attention and practice.

Riding the Air Blade at Step-Up Money

The Air Blade spends its premium on presence and ease. As an automatic, it removes shifting from the riding equation entirely, and its step-up positioning shows in a more substantial machine with a firmer road feel. Riders who want to blend low-effort control with a bit more confidence at speed will recognise what the extra tier buys within the first kilometre.

In city traffic the automatic layout does the same quiet work it does across the step-up tier: constant pace changes, tight gaps, and sudden stops are handled without gear decisions, which keeps attention where Hanoi demands it, on the flow around you. The flat 1,500,000 VND monthly rate also gives long-stay riders a fixed cost from day one, which no band-priced alternative can promise.

The honest question is whether that package earns 50,000 VND per day over the entry tier. For short stays and loose budgets, the answer is often why not. For longer stays the arithmetic gets sharper, because a month of Air Blade costs 1,500,000 VND flat while the Sirius band tops out at 1,200,000 VND, and the premium must justify itself against the whole trip, not just a single ride.

Running Costs on Top of the Rate

Running costs refuse to take sides in this comparison. Both engines are small and frugal city units, real-world fuel use falls in a similar range, and parking in central Hanoi is charged per bike rather than per tier. Whichever model you ride, the daily out-of-pocket costs beyond the rental rate look much the same.

Fuel arrangements are settled at the counter rather than chosen by model, so ask how the tank is handed over and returned before you ride off. The practical questions that come with any rental, deposits, documents, and what happens if something goes wrong, are answered in the rental FAQ, and they apply to both tiers equally.

One soft cost deserves a line of its own: learning time. The Air Blade is fully usable from the first minute by almost anyone, while the Sirius asks beginners for a day or two of practice before shifting stops demanding attention. On a ten-day trip, two cautious days are a fifth of your rental, a cost no price list shows but every rider pays or avoids.

Which Tier Fits Your Stay

Budget-driven stays resolve quickly. The Sirius wins the price column at every period, and its monthly band means a long stay can be secured for as little as 900,000 VND if the quote lands at the bottom. Riders who shift confidently, and travelers optimizing a fixed budget, should sign for the entry tier and spend the difference on the trip itself.

Comfort- and confidence-driven stays point the other way. Beginners, riders who want zero mechanical demands, and long-stay renters who value a flat, predictable monthly number all get something concrete from the Air Blade tier. When the monthly premium is 300,000 to 600,000 VND spread across thirty days, the daily cost of the upgrade is the price of a coffee, which reframes the decision for many riders.

The middle ground is a plain question of gears. If you can shift, the Sirius covers every practical need at the lower verified rate; if you cannot or do not want to, the Air Blade automates the whole experience at the higher one. The overview of Hanoi motorbike rental explains how periods and conditions work, and both models fit cleanly inside it.

Both machines are booked through Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu at 112 Nguyen Van Cu in Long Bien, Hanoi, and because they sit in different tiers with separate demand, asking about your dates for both models in one message is the efficient move. If the premium tier is sold out on your dates, the entry option covers the same city at a lower price, so a fallback is cheap insurance.

Confirm the numbers when you book. The daily and weekly rates for both models are fixed on the verified list, the Air Blade month is flat at 1,500,000 VND, and the Sirius month lands inside its 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND band, so ask exactly where your quote sits and what it includes. That single question closes every gap this comparison can leave open.

Summarised in one breath: the Sirius is the verified bargain at 150,000 VND daily, 700,000 VND weekly, and a 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND monthly band; the Air Blade is the verified upgrade at 200,000 VND daily, 1,000,000 VND weekly, and a flat 1,500,000 VND month. Pick the tier that matches your riding, confirm the current rates, and enjoy Hanoi either way.

Frequently asked questions about Sirius vs Air Blade rental costs in Hanoi

Which model is cheaper to rent in Hanoi?

The Yamaha Sirius, at every verified period: 150,000 VND versus 200,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND versus 1,000,000 VND per week, and a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND against the Air Blade flat 1,500,000 VND.

How much more does the Air Blade cost per week?

Exactly 300,000 VND more, verified on the published list: 1,000,000 VND for the Air Blade week against 700,000 VND for the Sirius week. That equals two full days of entry-tier rental, so weekly renters should weigh the upgrade deliberately.

How do the monthly rates compare?

The Sirius rents inside a verified band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, while the Air Blade carries a flat verified rate of 1,500,000 VND per month. The Air Blade number is fixed and predictable; the Sirius figure depends on your quote, so confirm both with the shop.

Is the Air Blade easier to ride than the Sirius?

For most riders, yes. The Air Blade is fully automatic, with no gears to shift, while the Sirius uses foot-shifted semi-automatic gearing. Beginners typically find the automatic layout usable immediately and the underbone format learnable over a day or two.

Do the two models differ much on fuel or parking?

No. Both are economical city machines, fuel consumption is similar in real traffic, and parking is charged per bike, not per tier. Running costs beyond the rental rate are close enough that the tier premium is the only real difference.

Is the Air Blade worth the upgrade for a month-long stay?

It depends on your priorities. The flat 1,500,000 VND month buys automatic ease and predictable budgeting, while the Sirius band tops out at 1,200,000 VND for riders happy to shift. Confirm current quotes and let your riding style decide.

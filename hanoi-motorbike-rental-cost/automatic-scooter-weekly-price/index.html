Automatic Scooter Weekly Rental Price in Hanoi: Cost Planning Guide

An automatic scooter rents by the week in Hanoi at one of two verified prices: 700,000 VND per week for the entry tier and 1,000,000 VND per week for the step-up tier. This guide plans a seven-day stay around both numbers, because the honest weekly question is not just what a week costs, but which tier the week actually needs.

We cover the two verified weekly rates, the daily averages they buy, when the week beats paying daily, when the month beats the week, what each tier buys across seven days, and the deposit, gear and support questions a week adds. The Two Verified Weekly Rates

An automatic scooter in Hanoi rents at 700,000 VND per week or 1,000,000 VND per week, and both figures come straight from the shop's published price list. Nothing in this guide pads them with invented extras, promotions or bundle discounts. The two numbers are the entire weekly story for twist-and-go machines.

The entry tier at 700,000 VND per week holds the Yamaha Mio and Honda Click, alongside the semi-automatic Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius at the same price. The step-up tier at 1,000,000 VND per week holds the Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike. The 50cc scooter publishes only a daily rate, 200,000 VND, so a 50cc week is quoted by the desk rather than estimated anywhere, including here.

Converted to daily averages, the two weeks tell the story. The entry week averages 100,000 VND per day against the 150,000 VND daily rate. The step-up week averages roughly 143,000 VND per day against 200,000 VND. The week is the structure where the fleet starts rewarding commitment, and both averages reward it immediately.

What the Week Saves

The savings against daily rates are clean arithmetic. In the entry tier, five daily rates total 750,000 VND, already past the 700,000 VND week, so the week wins at five days and saves 350,000 VND over a full seven paid daily. In the step-up tier, five daily rates total exactly 1,000,000 VND, matching the week, and every day after the fifth is money the week keeps.

Below five days the daily rate stays honest on both tiers. Two entry days cost 300,000 VND, three 450,000 VND, four 600,000 VND, all under the 700,000 VND week. Two step-up days cost 400,000 VND, three 600,000 VND, four 800,000 VND, all under the 1,000,000 VND week. The week is a tool with a hard edge at five days, and the honest planner respects the edge from both sides.

The weekly mistake is paying daily into a week out of inertia. Plans stretch, a three-day trip becomes a six-day one, and each extra daily rate quietly re-buys flexibility the plan no longer needs. Mark the fifth day as a decision point and the drift stops costing money.

A Week in the Entry Tier

The entry-tier week, 700,000 VND for a Yamaha Mio or Honda Click, is the cheapest fully published week in the fleet. What it buys is the honest city automatic across a full seven-day rhythm: light, low, easy to park, and simple enough that the whole learning curve fits in the first minute of the first day.

Seven days of city riding is exactly what the Mio and Click were kept for. The early days cover the Old Quarter lanes and Hoan Kiem loops, the middle days stretch to the Temple of Literature and West Lake, and the later days cross to Long Bien for the bridge views. Nothing in that week asks the entry tier for more than it has.

The entry-tier mistake is assuming the cheapest week is the lesser week. For a city-only plan the Mio and Click lose nothing to the step-up machines, and the 300,000 VND saved against a step-up week is real money in a Hanoi budget. The tier question is a plan-shape question, and a city-shaped plan answers it itself.

A Week in the Step-Up Tier

The step-up week, 1,000,000 VND, holds the Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike, three machines with three different reasons. The Vision is the storage and comfort choice, with room for a week of shopping and a settled ride. The Air Blade is the performance choice, with punch for faster traffic. The electric swaps fuel stops for charging, at a running cost near zero.

The step-up week earns its 300,000 VND premium when the seven days actually use what the tier buys. A week with longer lakeside runs, two-up riding, or shopping loads justifies the Vision. A week of confident merging and longer stretches justifies the Air Blade. A week of pure city errands flatters the electric, because its silence and simplicity compound across seven days.

The step-up mistake is paying the premium for a city-only week that the Mio or Click would carry just as well at 700,000 VND. The premium is a purchase, not a habit, and the honest weekly planner buys it only when the week calls for it.

When the Month Beats the Week

The week is the middle structure, and both tiers publish the months that beat it. The entry tier's month runs 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND, confirmed at the counter as a range. The step-up months run 1,500,000 VND for the Honda Air Blade and electric motorbike, and 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND for the Honda Vision.

The crossover math is dramatic on the entry tier. Two weeks booked weekly cost 1,400,000 VND against a monthly range that can start at 900,000 VND, so a stay stretching past ten days should be a monthly conversation long before the second week is paid. On the step-up tier, two weeks cost 2,000,000 VND against the Air Blade's 1,500,000 VND month, and the same rule holds.

The cautious-weekly mistake is booking weekly out of fear of the month, as if a monthly rate were a life sentence. A monthly agreement is a rate structure, and if the stay shortens the desk is the right place to restructure. Cautious weekly booking past ten days is the most expensive habit in the whole comparison. The Hanoi motorbike rental cost hub shows the full ladder for every model.

A Week of Automatic Riding in Hanoi

A week is the right length to live with the machine, and Hanoi rewards it. The honest weekly rhythm starts in the Old Quarter, where the light automatics make the narrow lanes effortless, moves outward to the Temple of Literature and the West Lake lanes, and finishes with a crossing to Long Bien for the bridge views and the market.

The automatic transmission is the week's quiet advantage. Seven days of stop-start traffic make constant simplicity a relief, and by the third day the twist-and-go delivery stops being a convenience and becomes the natural way the city moves. A week of fuel on a city automatic is a small line, and on the electric it disappears entirely into a charging routine.

The honest caveat is the out-of-town day. A week that includes a long highway run should match the machine to it, and the step-up tier is the better home for that day, or the plan should keep the week inside the city. The counter conversation is the moment to ask about a mid-week machine swap, because flexibility is a question, not an assumption.

Deposits, Gear and Support

A week adds weight to the standard questions. Ask what the deposit is, in what form it is held, and what has to be true at return for it to come back in full. Cash deposits get receipts and card holds get release dates, and a seven-day hold deserves the answers in writing.

Ask what is included across the whole stay: helmets for each rider, a lock, rain ponchos. Hanoi rain arrives fast and a week will likely meet it, so the poncho question stops being theoretical. Confirm the fuel arrangement at handover, full out and full back is the common pattern, and budget a small top-up for city distances.

Support is the question a week makes essential: the number to call if the machine has a problem, and the swap procedure if a check fails mid-week. A week is long enough for a puncture, and the answer should live in a pocket before it is needed. The rental FAQ collects the common answers on one page.

The week-long mistake is skipping the agreement. Read the one-page contract at the counter, in the shade, before riding, because every verbal answer about deposit, gear, fuel and support becomes a promise on that page, and two minutes of reading are the cheapest part of the week.

Choosing the Weekly Structure

The honest ladder is short. One to four days: pay daily, 150,000 or 200,000 VND depending on tier. Five to nine days: take the week, 700,000 VND entry or 1,000,000 VND step-up. Ten days and beyond: ask about the month, 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND entry or 1,500,000 to 2,000,000 VND step-up, and stop counting days.

Choose the tier by the week's shape. City-only weeks fit the entry tier at 700,000 VND. Weeks that need storage, comfort, performance or charging fit the step-up tier at 1,000,000 VND. The 300,000 VND difference is real money, but it is small money next to a structure error, so fix the structure first and the tier second.

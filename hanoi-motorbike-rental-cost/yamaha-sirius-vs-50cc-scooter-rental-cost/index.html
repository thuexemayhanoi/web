This pairing compares a machine with an engine class. The Yamaha Sirius is a specific semi-automatic underbone, the entry tier of the verified rental list, while the 50cc scooter is a category of small automatic machines whose appeal for visitors runs deeper than specifications. Comparing them means comparing both price and purpose.

The price cards differ in an unusual way. Both models publish a verified daily rate, the Sirius at 150,000 VND and the 50cc scooter at 200,000 VND, but the 50cc publishes nothing beyond the day. That asymmetry is not a gap in this guide; it is a fact of the published list, and it changes how the two options should be compared at every rental length.

The sections below read the price list exactly as published, explain what each side offers for its money, compare the riding experience of a geared underbone with a small automatic scooter, and set out which questions belong at the counter. Where a figure exists it is quoted; where none exists, the only honest answer is the shop itself.

The Verified Price List, Honestly Read

Start with what is printed. The Sirius rents at a verified 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND. The 50cc scooter rents at a verified 200,000 VND per day, and the published list carries no weekly or monthly figure for it, so anything longer than a day is a conversation with the shop rather than a number on a page.

That single difference structures the whole comparison. For a one-day rental, both options have prices and the choice is complete. For anything longer, the Sirius gains verified, published economics at every period, while the 50cc keeps only its daily rate, and stacking daily rates for a week costs 1,400,000 VND on paper before any shop adjustment. Long-stay comparisons therefore cannot be finished without the shop.

None of this makes the 50cc expensive in itself; it makes the 50cc differently priced, with the daily rate as the only published fact. Riders comparing the two honestly should carry both ideas at once: the Sirius is cheaper per day and fully priced for every period, and the 50cc costs more per day with an open question beyond it. The full tier structure is mapped in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

Where the Daily Rates Sit

On the numbers that exist, the Sirius wins the day by a clear 50,000 VND. A rider paying 150,000 VND for a Sirius against 200,000 VND for a 50cc scooter saves a third of the daily rate every day of the rental, and those savings are verified rather than estimated. At the daily level, the price column finishes the argument before any riding begins.

The gap also compounds in a specific, countable way. Two days of Sirius cost 300,000 VND against 400,000 VND for the 50cc; four days cost 600,000 VND against 800,000 VND. Because the 50cc side of the ledger only extends by stacking days, every extra day widens the difference in the Sirius favour by another 50,000 VND, at least until the shop says otherwise.

The counterweight is that the 50cc daily rate is not really competing on arithmetic. Its buyers are usually shopping for something other than the lowest price, a lighter, simpler, licence-friendly riding experience, and the premium is the cost of that particular peace of mind. The price comparison ends there and the purpose comparison takes over, which is where the next two sections go.

What the 50cc Class Is For

The 50cc class earns its premium through accessibility. Small automatic machines are typically the lightest, least intimidating things on two wheels, with no gears, modest speeds, and a riding experience beginners can manage from the first minute. For nervous first-timers, that gentleness is worth more than any daily-rate difference.

Licence considerations bring many visitors to this class in the first place. Rules around small-displacement machines are often more permissive than for larger bikes, and travellers who are unsure about their eligibility to ride in Vietnam frequently look at 50cc options for that reason. Requirements depend on your situation, so confirm what applies to you before riding anything, and treat the class as a starting point for that conversation.

What the class gives up is capability. A small-displacement scooter is built for compact city routes rather than confidence at speed, and long or ambitious rides are outside its comfort zone. Within the neighbourhoods, markets, and short hops of everyday Hanoi, that limitation barely shows; the moment the plan grows, the machine politely declines.

What the Sirius Does Better

The Sirius answers with capability at a lower price. Its verified 150,000 VND daily rate buys a machine that handles the city, carries a pillion without complaint, and holds its composure on the bigger roads beyond the centre. Riders with shifting confidence get a fuller machine for less money, and the published weekly and monthly rates make longer stays simple to budget.

Its format also carries the usual underbone strengths. The semi-automatic gearbox keeps the rider engaged without demanding clutch skills, the machine is a native of Vietnamese streets with mechanics everywhere, and petrol stations make its running costs completely routine. For riders who treat the bike as transport rather than therapy, the Sirius is the practical answer.

The cost of that practicality is involvement. Shifting through dense traffic demands attention until it becomes habit, and the underbone posture is more riderly than the seated ease of a small scooter. Riders who enjoy that engagement get it free; riders who do not are paying 50,000 VND less for an experience they may not want, which is no bargain at all.

Running Costs and Everyday Economics

On running costs, the two options are closer than their rental rates suggest. Both are light, economical machines whose real-world fuel or energy use in city riding stays modest, and neither carries daily expenses that shift the comparison on their own. Parking across central Hanoi is charged per bike, and both machines fit the same kerbside rows.

The Sirius adds one structural advantage: predictable long-stay economics. A verified weekly rate and a monthly band mean a week or a month of riding can be priced before arrival, with fuel as the only open line. The 50cc side, with only a daily rate published, keeps every long-stay budget provisional until the shop quotes, which matters for travellers who plan totals in advance.

Fuel and charging arrangements are settled at the counter for both machines, so confirm how the tank or battery is handed over and returned. The practical questions around deposits, documents, and rental conditions are shared territory and are answered in the rental FAQ. Beyond the rental rate itself, the everyday economics of the two options rarely decide the choice.

Matching the Machine to Your Stay

Short city stays with a confident rider resolve toward the Sirius. It is cheaper per day, priced for the week if the stay extends, and more capable if the itinerary grows beyond a few blocks. Riders who can shift, or who are willing to learn over a day or two, get more machine for less money at every period that has a published figure.

Short stays with a hesitant rider tilt toward the 50cc. Its automatic delivery, gentle speeds, and licence-friendly class are exactly what many first-time visitors want from a first rental, and the premium buys down anxiety rather than performance. If the alternative is not riding at all, 50,000 VND a day is a small price for the confidence to join the traffic.

Long stays belong to a conversation rather than a rule. The Sirius carries verified monthly economics in its 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND band, while the 50cc carries an open question and a stack of daily rates. Compare the two honestly only after asking the shop, and read the overview of Hanoi motorbike rental for how rental periods work across the fleet.

In summary: the Sirius is the verified value option at 150,000 VND per day with published weekly and monthly rates, and the 50cc scooter is the verified accessibility option at 200,000 VND per day with longer periods quoted by the shop. One is priced for economy and capability, the other for ease and entry, and the right question at the counter will pick the winner for your stay.

Frequently asked questions about Sirius vs 50cc scooter rental costs in Hanoi

Which is cheaper to rent in Hanoi, the Sirius or a 50cc scooter?

The Sirius, on every published figure. It rents at 150,000 VND per day against 200,000 VND for the 50cc scooter, and it also carries verified weekly and monthly rates that the 50cc list does not publish at all.

Does the 50cc scooter have a weekly or monthly rental rate?

The published price list shows a daily rate only, 200,000 VND, with no weekly or monthly figure for the 50cc scooter. Anything beyond a day is quoted by the shop directly, so ask for an extended-rental price before budgeting a longer stay.

Why does the smaller 50cc scooter cost more per day than the Sirius?

Because the price list reflects positioning rather than engine size. The Sirius is the entry tier price anchor at 150,000 VND per day, while the 50cc scooter is priced at 200,000 VND for its class, an easy, licence-friendly category that many visitors specifically look for.

Do I need a licence to ride a 50cc scooter in Vietnam?

Rules for small-displacement machines are often more permissive than for larger bikes, which is part of the class appeal, but requirements depend on your nationality and situation. Confirm what applies to you before riding anything, and ask the shop when you book.

Which machine is easier for a complete beginner?

The 50cc scooter. It is fully automatic, light, and gentle, with modest speeds that make early riding manageable. The Sirius uses foot-shifted semi-automatic gearing, which is learnable but adds workload during the first days in traffic.

Which is the better choice for a longer stay?

The Sirius is the only one of the two with published long-stay economics, a verified weekly rate and a monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND. A long 50cc rental depends on a shop quote, so ask about both numbers before deciding.

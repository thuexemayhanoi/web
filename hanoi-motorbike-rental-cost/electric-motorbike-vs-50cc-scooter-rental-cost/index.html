Electric Motorbike vs 50cc Scooter: Hanoi Rental Cost Comparison

Two machines sit at the easy-entry end of this fleet: the electric motorbike and the 50cc scooter, both rented at a verified 200,000 VND per day at Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu. The identical daily price makes them natural alternatives for renters who want the simplest possible ride, and it makes this comparison unusual, because the cost question cannot decide a single day of it.

This guide compares the two rate cards with verified figures only: the electric motorbike’s 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week and flat 1,500,000 VND per month, and the 50cc class’s single published daily rate, with everything beyond it treated as a question for the counter rather than a blank to fill. It builds on the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost, the main Hanoi motorbike rental resource, and the rental FAQ. One tie, one complete card, one honest asymmetry.

Renters who want the simplest possible machine in Hanoi tend to arrive at the same two doors: the electric motorbike and the 50cc scooter. One removes gears and petrol station visits from the riding day, the other keeps things small and familiar. At Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu both doors open at the same verified price, 200,000 VND per day, which makes this the rare comparison where the entry cost cannot break the tie.

The tie breaks on the shape of the rate card rather than the size of the rate. The electric card continues past the day into a published weekly tier of 1,000,000 VND and a flat monthly tier of 1,500,000 VND. The 50cc card publishes a daily rate and nothing beyond it, so a renter planning more than a few days is comparing one machine whose longer costs are on paper and one whose longer costs live entirely at the counter.

This guide compares the two cards with verified figures only. It does not speculate about running costs, charging times, speeds or fuel economy, none of which appear on a rental rate card, and it does not fill the 50cc card’s empty rows with arithmetic. What it does is place the one verified number both machines share next to the numbers only one of them has, and let the length of your stay pick the winner.

The verified rates side by side

The electric motorbike’s card reads 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week and 1,500,000 VND per month, the monthly figure quoted flat. It is a complete card, three tiers deep, with the month fixed rather than ranged, which means a long-stay renter can budget the full price of the machine before leaving home.

The 50cc card reads 200,000 VND per day, and that is the whole of what is published. There is no verified weekly rate, no verified monthly rate, and this guide will not manufacture either by multiplying the daily figure out. Any longer-term 50cc arrangement is a counter conversation, with the shop quoting what it offers for the dates in question.

Side by side, the comparison has a clean shape: identical at the day, divergent in structure everywhere after. Both machines sit in the 200,000 VND daily tier alongside the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade, above the 150,000 VND entry tier of the Honda Click, Yamaha Mio, Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius, and the full fleet context for those tiers is charted in the guide to Hanoi motorbike rental cost.

What the same daily price decides

The same daily price makes the short-stay comparison purely about the machines. An electric motorbike and a 50cc scooter are different answers to the same question, what is the easiest thing to ride in this city, and the 200,000 VND daily rate charges nothing extra for either answer. A renter weighing them for a weekend is weighing character, not cost.

The practical differences are the ones to ask about rather than assume. How each machine fits your routes and plans, what suits your build, and the licence considerations that bring many visitors to these two classes in the first place: whether a given class suits your situation depends on the licence you hold and where it was issued, and that is a fact about you, not about either machine, so confirm it for your own case.

None of those questions has a price on either card. What does have a price is time: the moment the rental stretches past a few days, the electric card keeps answering with published numbers while the 50cc card goes quiet, and the comparison stops being about riding character and starts being about which card you can plan on.

Where the 50cc card stops

Reading the 50cc card honestly means accepting its brevity. A single published figure is not a hidden fee or a trap; it is a card that stops at the day, and the correct response is to ask the shop directly about anything longer. The wrong response is arithmetic: multiplying 200,000 VND by seven or thirty produces a number that resembles a weekly or monthly rate but is neither, because no such rate has been quoted by anyone with the authority to quote it.

This matters more in this pairing than in most, because the temptation is strong. The daily tie invites renters to assume the rest of the cards match too, and the empty rows say nothing to contradict them, since empty is silent rather than equal. The only honest filler for a silent row is the shop’s answer for your dates.

The 50cc class earns its place in this comparison on the daily row, where it costs exactly what the electric motorbike costs, and on whatever the counter offers beyond it, which this guide does not guess at. Renters who love the class lose nothing by asking; renters who plan without asking are not planning, they are hoping, and hoping is not a budget.

The electric card keeps going

The electric card’s weekly and monthly tiers give it something no other machine shares with the 50cc class: a published trajectory. The week at 1,000,000 VND and the flat month at 1,500,000 VND mean the machine’s cost is known at every horizon the shop rents across, and the flat monthly figure means it is known without a confirmation call.

The monthly tier is also the best value on the card. Four stacked weeks of the electric motorbike cost 4,000,000 VND at the published weekly rate, so a month at 1,500,000 VND flat saves 2,500,000 VND against the machine’s own week, the same kind of step-down every published monthly rate in the fleet offers relative to its weekly rate.

For a renter choosing between the two doors, that trajectory is the practical difference. Behind the identical daily price, the electric motorbike is a machine you can rent for an afternoon, a week or a month on numbers you already have, and the 50cc scooter is a machine you can rent for an afternoon on numbers you have and for longer on numbers you must go and get.

Matching the card to your stay

A few days: the tie holds, both cards bill 200,000 VND per day, and the decision belongs to riding character, fit and availability. Ask to see both machines, confirm what applies to your licence situation, and choose the one that suits the riding you have planned; price gives you no help and asks for none.

A week or two: the comparison tips toward the card that exists. The electric week is a published 1,000,000 VND, while the 50cc week is a question with an answer that lives at the counter and may differ by rental. A renter who needs the number before committing has it on one card only, and that is the electric card.

A month or longer: the tip becomes a lean. The electric month is 1,500,000 VND flat, budgetable today, while the 50cc month is a conversation that has not happened yet. Renters set on the 50cc class for a long stay should have that conversation early and in person, and renters who want a firm figure for planning will find it on the electric card. Wider long-stay planning context sits in the main Hanoi motorbike rental resource.

The counter conversation

The counter conversation covers both machines efficiently. For the 50cc class, ask what the shop offers beyond the published daily rate for your intended duration, if it offers anything, and at what price, and ask early enough that the answer can inform the choice rather than follow it.

For the electric motorbike, the card answers most questions before they are asked, but availability is not one of them, so confirm the machine for your dates, and ask the same logistics questions that apply to any rental: the documents and deposit the shop expects, and what applies to your licence situation. The general answers are collected in the rental FAQ, and your specific case is a five-minute conversation.

The counter is at Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu, 112 Nguyen Van Cu, Long Bien, Hanoi, open daily from 09:00 to 21:00. Ask to see both machines when both are available, put the 50cc long-stay question in words, and let the electric card’s published tiers compete with whatever answer comes back. That is the whole comparison, run honestly, in one visit.

A tie with one honest asymmetry

Set plainly, this comparison is a tie that becomes an asymmetry. At 200,000 VND per day the electric motorbike and the 50cc scooter are the same rental priced twice, and a short-stay renter loses nothing by choosing either on pure preference. The moment the stay grows, the electric card continues answering with published weekly and monthly tiers while the 50cc card hands the question to the counter.

Neither machine wins on the daily row, because neither can: the numbers are identical. The electric motorbike wins the planning contest for any renter who needs the whole cost known in advance, and the 50cc class keeps every other advantage it had before this guide was written, with its longer-term pricing available wherever the shop says it is, at whatever the shop says it costs.

The disciplined summary: rent either machine by the day on preference, rent the electric motorbike by the week or month on its published card, and rent the 50cc for longer only on numbers the shop has actually given you. Verified figures, one honest question mark, and a tie that the calendar, not the price list, breaks.

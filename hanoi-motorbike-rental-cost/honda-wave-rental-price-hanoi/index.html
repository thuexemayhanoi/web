Honda Wave Rental Price in Hanoi

The Honda Wave is the most common motorbike on Vietnamese roads, and it is also the cheapest bike category in our published rental list. Riders choose it because it is light, simple and cheap to run, which matters when you are riding through Hanoi traffic every day of your trip. This guide covers the verified Honda Wave rental price in Hanoi for every rental period, how the rates compare with the other models we rent, and how to decide whether the Wave is the right bike for you.

Why the Honda Wave is so popular in Vietnam

The Wave has been sold in Vietnam for decades, and you will see it everywhere: delivery riders, families taking children to school, shop owners moving stock, and farmers carrying produce from the countryside. That popularity has practical consequences for renters. Spare parts are easy to find, every mechanic in the country knows the bike, and the riding position is familiar to anyone who has spent time in Vietnam before.

The Wave is also light. A lighter bike is easier to push, park and pick up, which is a real advantage in a city where parking often means squeezing into a marked strip of pavement. The seat height suits most riders, and the upright riding position keeps your view above the traffic around you. For first-time riders in Vietnam, that light weight is one of the most reassuring features a rental bike can have.

There is a cultural argument for the Wave as well: when you ride the same bike that millions of locals ride, you blend in. You are not the tourist on an oversized machine; you are simply another rider in the flow, which tends to make daily riding in the Old Quarter and surrounding districts feel less intimidating. How daily, weekly and monthly rates compare

The weekly and monthly rates are much lower than stacking daily rentals. Renting a Honda Wave by the day for seven days would cost 1,050,000 VND, while the weekly rate is 700,000 VND. That is a saving of 350,000 VND in a single week, which is significant on a backpacker budget.

The gap grows with time. Thirty daily rentals would cost 4,500,000 VND, while the monthly Honda Wave rate is between 900,000 and 1,200,000 VND. In other words, a month on a Wave costs roughly a quarter of what daily rentals would cost. Even the top of the monthly range is less than a fifth of the stacked daily price.

A useful way to compare periods is to look at the cost per day. The daily rate is 150,000 VND. The weekly rate works out to 100,000 VND per day. The monthly rate works out to between 30,000 and 40,000 VND per day. The longer the period, the cheaper each day of riding becomes, and the difference is large enough that choosing the right period usually matters more than choosing between the two or three cheapest models. Honda Wave versus other rental bikes

The Wave sits in the cheapest rental tier, alongside the Yamaha Sirius. Both are semi-automatic bikes renting for 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000–1,200,000 VND per month. The main difference between them is brand and styling rather than price; the Sirius is Yamaha's answer to the same utility brief, and the choice between the two usually comes down to which one is available and which feels better when you sit on it.

If you want an automatic scooter instead of a semi-automatic, the Yamaha Mio and Honda Click rent for the same 150,000 VND per day, while the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade rent for 200,000 VND per day. The 50cc machines and the electric motorbike also rent for 200,000 VND per day. For a full comparison of automatic models, read the Hanoi scooter rental hub, and for the lightest category see the 50cc motorbike rental guide.

Comparing categories is genuinely useful because the daily difference between a Wave and a Vision is only 50,000 VND, but over a month the published ranges diverge more, so the period you rent for changes which bike is the rational choice. A rider staying a month could either take the cheapest tier and save, or spend the difference on an automatic for comfort. Both decisions are defensible; what matters is that you make the comparison with real numbers before you arrive.

What riding a semi-automatic actually feels like

A semi-automatic bike has a foot lever for gears but no hand clutch. You roll off the throttle, tap the lever with your left foot, and roll back on. The left hand does nothing except brake and steer, which surprises riders who learned on manual bikes. Most riders get comfortable with the rhythm within a day, and the Wave in particular has a forgiving gearbox that tolerates lazy shifts and low-speed maneuvers.

The advantage over a fully manual bike is obvious: no clutch control at junctions, no stalling when traffic restarts. The disadvantage versus an automatic scooter is that you still shift. In stop-and-go Old Quarter traffic, some riders prefer not to think about gears at all. That is a personal preference, not a safety question, and both categories are rented to beginners every day.

Braking deserves a note. Like most underbone bikes, the Wave brakes with a front disc and a rear drum, and riders in Vietnam use both brakes together smoothly rather than grabbing the front lever alone. Wet painted road markings and manhole covers become slippery in the rain, so the standard advice is to brake earlier and lighter than you would on dry Western roads. These habits apply to every bike you can rent here, not just the Wave.

Everyday running: fuel, storage and trips

The Wave is a small-capacity underbone, which means fuel costs stay modest over a normal day of city riding. Fuel stations are dense in Hanoi and along the main highways out of the city, so range anxiety is rarely a factor for day trips in the capital. For everything about fueling, station etiquette and planning fuel for longer rides, see the general guidance in our motorbike guides hub.

Storage is minimal: a small under-seat or rear rack space for a rain jacket, a lock and a backpack. The Wave is not the bike for two full-sized suitcases, but that is true of every bike in the fleet. Riders carrying more gear usually plan around the bike rather than choosing a bigger machine, because the price gap between tiers is small while the riding advantages of a light bike in Hanoi are constant.

For trips beyond the city, the Wave is a credible choice on the main paved routes: the engine is built for sustained everyday riding, and the bike is light enough to manage comfortably at the speeds typical of provincial roads. Who should rent a Honda Wave

The Wave is the right choice when budget and simplicity matter more than comfort features. It suits riders who plan to cover real distance, because the engine is built for everyday utility rather than showroom appeal, and it suits anyone who wants the cheapest verified weekly or monthly rate in the fleet.

It is less suitable for riders who want a fully automatic experience, a larger seat or more storage. An automatic scooter such as the Click or Vision is easier to operate, while the Air Blade offers a bigger body for the same daily rate as the Vision. None of these choices is unsafe; they are about comfort and budget. When in doubt, ask to see the actual bikes available for your dates before deciding, and ride the one that fits rather than the one that photographs well.

The Wave also earns its place for longer stays. Renting the cheapest tier for a month keeps your total transport cost low while leaving budget for everything else in Hanoi, and for many long-stay visitors that trade is exactly what they want.

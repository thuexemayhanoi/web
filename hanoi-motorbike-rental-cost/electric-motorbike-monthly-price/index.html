Electric Motorbike Monthly Rental Price in Hanoi: How to Request a Quote

The electric motorbike rents by the month in Hanoi, but its monthly price is not published: the shop gives it on request, and the deposit is confirmed by model. Here the electric's monthly rental is explained: what to ask when requesting a quote, what a month on the electric is like, what to check in a long agreement, and how to compare the quote with the published petrol-scooter monthly prices.

Daily and weekly prices for the electric are also given on request, so ask for the period that matches your stay, and ask about the deposit, gear, charging and maintenance before a long-term rental. For other models and prices, see the Hanoi motorbike rental cost guide and the rental FAQ.

The monthly price is given on request, so contact the shop with your dates and the model you want, and ask for a figure for the whole month. Do not assume promotions, package deals or discounts for longer rentals; the quote you receive is the planning number.

A monthly quote is easiest to judge next to the shorter periods, so ask for the weekly and daily rate for the same machine at the same time. With all three figures for your exact dates, you can see which period suits your stay.

To judge a monthly quote fairly, set it against the published petrol-scooter prices for a month, with the same dates, the same deposit questions and the same included gear on both sides. The Wave, Sirius, Mio and Click cost 900,000-1,200,000 VND a month.

A Month with the Electric

The electric motorbike is the quiet choice of the fleet, built for exactly the kind of riding a month in Hanoi demands: daily city lanes, commute-style hops, market runs and loops around the lakes. A month is the period where a machine's character becomes real, and the electric's character is calm: silent running that changes the feel of early mornings, instant gentle delivery that makes stop-start traffic almost relaxing, and no fuel stops at all.

A month also reveals what a day cannot. The electric carries a backpack and modest shopping comfortably, takes a passenger for hops across town, and asks nothing of its rider in gears, clutch or petrol queues. It is not the machine for long highway days, and riders planning weekend long-distance loops should say so at the shop, because the monthly agreement should match the real plan, not the brochure plan.

In short, for a month-long renter the electric suits riders who want the simplest, calmest month of city riding, with charging to arrange instead of a fuel budget.

Planning a Monthly Budget

Plan the month from four lines: the rental, the charging arrangement, maintenance expectations and daily small costs. The rental line is the figure the shop quotes for your dates. Charging on a city month adds little to the budget, but ask how it is arranged. Parking, where paid lots are used, is typically a few thousand VND per stop.

A month in Hanoi on an electric usually means a rhythm, not a schedule: morning coffee runs, Old Quarter evenings, a weekly big-market trip, occasional loops to West Lake or across Long Bien bridge. Plan the budget around that rhythm and the monthly rate quietly absorbs every ordinary day at a marginal cost close to nothing.

Rest days cost nothing extra on a monthly rate. Once the month is paid, rainy days, work days in cafes or a trip out of town add nothing to the rental. That slack is the main advantage of a monthly rental over shorter periods.

The budgeting mistake is treating the month as thirty riding days. Budget for about twenty riding days and let the month cover the rest; a plan like that holds up when real life gets in the way.

Comparing the Monthly Quote with Weekly Booking

Because the electric's weekly price is also given on request, ask the shop to quote both the month and the weeks for your dates. Set four weeks of the weekly quote against the monthly quote, and the comparison shows which structure suits a long stay.

Ask the shop at what length of stay the monthly quote becomes the better choice. If your stay may run past a single week, check the monthly price before you pay for a second week.

The comparison mistake is rolling week to week out of habit. Weekly booking feels flexible, but on a long stay it can cost more than a month, so compare the quotes before the second week begins.

Comparing with Daily Rental

Daily rental is not a plan for anything resembling a month. Ask for the electric's daily price along with the monthly quote, and the shop can show you where daily rental stops making sense for your dates.

For the electric, the right period depends on the quotes for your dates, so ask for all three at once. The Hanoi motorbike rental cost hub lays out the published prices for the other models in the fleet.

Electric Versus Other Monthly Rates

The Honda Air Blade rents at a published 1,500,000 VND a month. Against it, the electric offers silent running in place of petrol performance, and its monthly quote shows what that difference costs. The Honda Vision rents at 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND a month (confirm with the shop) and gives the most comfortable, well-finished ride with the largest storage space.

The entry tier has the lowest published monthly prices. The Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Yamaha Mio and Honda Click all rent at a verified 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month. Over a month, how well the machine fits matters more than first impressions, so ask whether your plan really suits the electric's strengths: short city loops, quiet riding and no fuel stops.

There is also the 50cc option for riders who want the smallest petrol engine class, at a verified 200,000 VND per day. Monthly prices for a 50cc machine are not published, so ask the shop for a figure for your dates.

The monthly comparison mistake is paying for riding the electric cannot do. A month that includes long highway weekends is petrol work, so compare the electric quote with the petrol prices for that kind of month. Choose the machine for the riding you actually do, not for the brochure in your head.

Long-Term Agreements, Deposits and Support

A monthly rental is a relationship, and the agreement should be read with more care than a daily one. The electric deposit is confirmed by model, so ask for the amount for the machine you will ride, then confirm its form and the return conditions in writing. Confirm what happens if the machine has a problem mid-month: the number to call, the swap procedure, and any downtime expectations. A month is long enough for a puncture, so the support questions are practical, not paranoid.

Confirm the charging boundaries before signing. Ask how the machine charges, where, what a day of city riding does to the battery, and what the shop expects at return. These are standard questions for any long-term electric rental, and the answers take a few minutes at the shop.

Confirm the gear: helmets for each rider, a lock, rain ponchos. A month in Hanoi will include rain, and included gear beats improvised gear every time. Inclusion is a question, never an assumption.

The long-term mistake is treating the month as an unattended rental. Inspect the electric at pickup, take photos of its condition and look it over each week, because spotting a small problem early keeps the month on budget and protects your deposit.

When the Month Is the Wrong Rate

For a short stay, a month on the electric is usually the wrong structure; ask for the daily and weekly quotes for your dates instead. The month is also a poor choice if your plans may change; if you might only stay a week, rent for a week and ask the shop how to extend.

For a long stay, whether you are a newly arrived expat, on a month-long course, working here for a while, or a slow traveller using Hanoi as a base, ask for the monthly quote first, and the quiet keeps paying off every morning.

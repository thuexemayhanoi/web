Honda Click vs Yamaha Mio in Hanoi

Below the premium automatics sit the two bikes that do most of the daily work of this city: the Honda Click and the Yamaha Mio. They are the budget tier of our scooter fleet, and they are the machines most renters actually choose, because they solve the everyday problem of Hanoi riding at the lowest verified price we offer. This guide compares them honestly, with the prices first and the character second.

The short version of the comparison is unusual: the two bikes are financially identical at every rental period, so the choice between them is purely about feel, fit and availability. The longer version is below, and every figure in it can be re-checked on the Honda Click rental price page, the Yamaha Mio rental price page and the rental cost hub.

The verified prices, side by side

Both bikes rent at exactly the same verified figures. The Honda Click is 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month. The Yamaha Mio is 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week, and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month. There is no price dimension to this comparison at any rental length, which makes it the simplest decision in the fleet.

For context, the tier above begins at 200,000 VND per day with the Honda Vision and the Air Blade, so choosing the budget tier saves 50,000 VND per day against the premium automatics. The semi-automatics, the Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius, share the 150,000 VND daily and 700,000 VND weekly figures with the Click and Mio, and our Wave vs Sirius comparison covers that pair if you are also weighing the semi-automatic format.

The monthly range deserves one line of explanation before it appears later in the guide: 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND is the verified range for this tier, and the exact figure within it is confirmed with us for your dates. That is a five-day span of possible figures, and for a month-long rental it is worth the one question that pins it down.

Why identical pricing simplifies the choice

When two products cost the same at every duration, the comparison collapses to what remains: character, fit and availability. That is genuinely good news for renters, because the remaining questions are the ones you can answer by sitting on the bikes for two minutes rather than by reading a spreadsheet. It also means there is no wrong financial answer here, a sentence that cannot be written about many comparisons.

The weekly arithmetic is worth seeing once. Seven stacked daily rentals on either bike would cost 1,050,000 VND, against the 700,000 VND weekly rate, so the week saves 350,000 VND on either machine. A month of stacked weeks would cost 2,800,000 VND, against the 900,000-1,200,000 VND monthly range, so the month is where this tier becomes genuinely inexpensive transport by any standard.

The practical advice that follows from all of this: do not research your way to a decision between these two bikes. Ask us what is available for your dates, come sit on both if both are free, and let ninety seconds of sitting decide what a hundred pages of specification sheets can only speculate about.

The Honda Click character

The Honda Click is the machine that made the small automatic scooter the default vehicle of urban Southeast Asia, and the reason is legibility: it is a friendly, undemanding bike that does exactly what a new rider expects. The controls are light, the seating position is natural, the under-seat storage takes a helmet or a day's shopping, and nothing about its behavior ever surprises a rider who has been on it for ten minutes.

In character terms the Click is calm and tidy. It accelerates predictably, holds its line without fuss, and treats its rider as a person with somewhere to be rather than a pilot with something to prove. For first-time riders in Vietnamese traffic, that predictability is worth more than any specification, because the first hour of riding in Hanoi asks a lot of a newcomer and the bike should not be adding to the questions.

The Click is also the bike most returning renters ask for by name, which says something that no specification sheet can. A machine that customers come back for, at the same price as its direct rival, is a machine that has answered this comparison for a lot of people already, and its reputation in that role is well earned.

The Yamaha Mio character

The Yamaha Mio is the Click's friendly opposite number: the same job description executed with a slightly different personality. Where the Click is tidy, the Mio is playful; where the Click is calm, the Mio is a touch more eager. Riders who prefer it usually describe it with words like light and easy, and the difference is real enough that people notice it within a block of riding.

The Mio's practical virtues are the same as the Click's, because the format demands them: automatic transmission, a step-through frame, storage under the seat, light controls and easy parking. The differences are in the seasoning rather than the recipe, and neither bike makes the other one look bad; they make each other look like alternatives, which is exactly what they are.

The honest thing to say about the character comparison is that it is a preference, not a ranking. Riders who have tried both defend their favorite with real conviction and concede the other is a fine machine, and after a decade of that argument in every rental shop in the region, the debate remains unresolved because it was never a factual question in the first place. Sit on both; you will know.

How they handle Hanoi's streets

Hanoi's riding environment is where this tier earns its popularity. The small automatic format is the correct tool for the city's densest districts: light enough to thread the Old Quarter's lanes, small enough to park where parking exists, and undemanding enough that the rider's attention can go to the traffic, where it belongs. Both the Click and the Mio are exactly this format, and both do the city job excellently.

The trade-off, stated as honestly as the strengths: this tier is built for the city and the shorter day trips, not for sustained high-speed riding. On the ring roads both bikes are comfortable at ordinary urban speeds, and on day trips out of the city they are pleasant companions on the quieter roads, but riders planning long-distance touring or sustained riding with a passenger should consider the larger automatics or the semi-automatics instead. Our scooter vs semi-automatic guide covers that format question in detail.

For the riding most visitors actually do, city exploration, evening rides, short trips to the lakes and the markets, this tier is not a compromise but the correct choice, and the extra size and presence of the premium tier would buy little that the lanes of the Old Quarter have room for. Choose the tier by the riding you will actually do, and this tier wins that contest more often than visitors expect.

Longer stays and the monthly range

For a month-long rental, the shared 900,000-1,200,000 VND range is one of the best value propositions in the fleet, and the model choice does not change it. Confirm the exact figure with us for your dates, and the comparison between the Click and Mio remains what it always was: a preference between two excellent machines at one price.

The monthly question for most renters is actually tier, not model. A month on this tier costs 900,000-1,200,000 VND; a month on the premium tier costs 1,500,000 to 2,000,000 VND depending on the model; and the question of whether the larger machines are worth the difference is covered honestly on the monthly rental hub. For pure city riding over a long stay, this tier's monthly arithmetic is hard to beat.

Long-stay riders should also read the expat rental guide, which explains how monthly arrangements work in practice, what we handle on the maintenance side, and the questions worth agreeing before a long rental begins. The Click and the Mio are the backbone of that part of our business, and for good reason.

Frequently asked questions about the Click and Mio

Which is cheaper, the Honda Click or the Yamaha Mio?

Neither. Both rent at 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000-1,200,000 VND per month, with the monthly figure confirmed for your dates. The choice between them is about feel and availability, not price.

Which bike is better for a first-time rider in Hanoi?

Both are excellent first bikes: light, automatic, predictable and easy to park. The Click is the marginally more familiar name for many visitors, but the honest advice is to sit on both if both are available and choose the one that feels natural, because both are correct answers.

Is this tier good enough for day trips out of Hanoi?

For the quieter roads and shorter day trips, yes, comfortably. For long-distance touring, sustained ring-road riding or regular two-up travel, the larger automatics or the semi-automatics suit better. Choose the tier by the riding you will actually do, and ask us if you are unsure.

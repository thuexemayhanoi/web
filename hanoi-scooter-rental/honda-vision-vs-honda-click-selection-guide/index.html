Hanoi Scooter Rental: Honda Vision vs Honda Click Selection Guide

The Honda Vision and the Honda Click are two of the most rented automatic scooters in Hanoi, and riders compare them constantly because they sit so close in size, price and purpose. Both are light, both are easy to ride, both park anywhere, and both cost far less per month than a motorbike taxi habit. The right choice depends on details: how far you ride each day, what you carry, who rides with you and how the verified rates stack up for your pattern.

This guide compares the two models head to head for Hanoi scooter rental customers. It sits alongside the Hanoi motorbike rental cost overview, the monthly motorbike rental Hanoi guide, and the model-specific pages on the Vision for city riding and the Click for city riding.

What this comparison covers

First, scope. The Vision and the Click are both small Honda automatics with similar top speeds, similar ease of use and the same basic layout: flat footboard, under-seat storage, twist-and-go transmission. Neither is a performance machine and neither pretends to be. The differences that matter to a renter are comfort, storage, ride quality over Hanoi's patchwork surfaces and, above all, price, because the two models sit in different rate bands at this operator.

Second, method. This comparison uses the operator's verified published rates, general knowledge of the two model lines and cautious, experience-based guidance about riding in Hanoi. It invents nothing: no estimated top speeds presented as facts, no claimed fuel consumption, no invented policies. Where something depends on the specific unit or on the shop's current practice, the answer is to ask at the counter, and the shop is open daily from 09:00 to 21:00 at 112 Nguyen Van Cu, Long Bien, Hanoi.

Third, fit. The best scooter is the one that matches your real week: your distances, your cargo, your passengers and your budget. A rider hopping between nearby points makes one choice; a rider crossing districts daily makes another; a rider carrying a second helmet and a laptop bag makes a third. Keep your own pattern in mind through every section below, because the comparison only becomes a decision when it meets your actual routine.

Where the Honda Vision wins

The Vision is tuned for smoothness. Its engine and transmission favour gentle, quiet low-speed running, which in Hanoi traffic is most of your riding, and its suspension and seat strike most riders as the more comfortable of the two over broken surfaces and long slow stretches. If your day is a chain of short urban hops, traffic-light starts and dense neighbourhood streets, the Vision's calm character is a genuine daily pleasure rather than a spec-sheet abstraction.

The Vision also carries well for its size. Its under-seat storage is generous for the class, easily holding a helmet, a rain poncho and a day's extras, and its light, neutral steering makes it effortless in tight parking rows and crowded lanes. Riders who move between many stops in a day, and who value the machine disappearing beneath them, consistently rate the Vision highly for exactly this kind of invisible competence.

Against all that, note the price: the Vision rents at 200,000 VND per day and 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month at this operator, which places it in the upper band for small automatics here. You pay that difference for comfort and polish. Whether it is worth it is genuinely a personal question, and the monthly arithmetic section below shows exactly when it is and when it is not.

Where the Honda Click wins

The Click wins first on price. It rents at 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, which makes it one of the most economical ways to move around Hanoi on your own wheels. For students, short-stay visitors, riders on a tight monthly budget and anyone whose trips are short and central, the Click's rate card is not a detail; it is the headline.

The Click also wins on simplicity. It is light, narrow and unfussy, easy to push, easy to park in packed rows, and easy to manage for newer riders building confidence. Its compact size suits the Old Quarter's lanes and sidewalk parking better than anything larger, and its controls are as straightforward as automatic scooters get. For pure urban threading, low speeds and short hops, the Click is close to the ideal tool.

Be clear about the trade: the Click gives up some comfort and some equipment compared with the Vision. Over long daily distances, rougher patches and two-up riding, the differences show up as more buzz, firmer seat feel and less carrying room. None of that is disqualifying; it simply means the Click is the right answer for short and central riding patterns, and merely a possible answer for everything else.

Verified rental rates for both models

At Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu, the Honda Vision is 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week and 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month. The Honda Click is 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month. Those are the operator's published figures, and they are the only numbers you should use for planning; unverified rate lists circulate online and age badly.

Read the gaps carefully. On daily rates the Vision costs 50,000 VND more per day; on weekly rates the difference is 300,000 VND; on monthly rates the bands differ by roughly 600,000 to 1,100,000 VND depending on where within each band your agreement sits. The monthly gap is the one that matters for residents, because it recurs every month and it is large enough to buy a good helmet, a poncho and a lock with change left over.

One practical note: the Vision's monthly rate is a band, 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND, and the Click's monthly rate is also a band, 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND. Where your agreement falls within a band can depend on the length of the commitment and the unit, so ask at the counter for the current monthly figure for the specific model before you decide. The question takes a minute and settles the single biggest variable in the comparison.

Monthly arithmetic for daily riders

For a five-day commuter, the Click's monthly band of 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND translates to roughly 45,000 VND per riding day at the top of the band, and less at the bottom. The Vision's 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND band works out to roughly 90,000 to 100,000 VND per riding day on the same schedule. That is a difference of several hundred thousand dong a month for the same commute, and it is the honest cost of the Vision's added comfort and storage.

The decision rule that falls out of the arithmetic is simple. If you ride most days and your routes are short, central and solo, the Click is the rational choice, and the money you save compounds month after month. If your days are long, your routes cross bridges and districts, you often carry a passenger or a full bag, or you simply ride enough that comfort is a daily quality-of-life item, the Vision's premium is money spent on the hours you actually spend on the road.

A hybrid path also exists and many riders take it: rent the cheaper Click for a first week or month while you learn the city, then move up once you know your real pattern. Learning your true distances and cargo needs costs less on the Click, and the upgrade decision is then made on data rather than on a brochure. There is no wrong answer between these two machines; there is only a right answer for your actual week.

Storage, passengers and practicality

Storage separates them more than riders expect. The Vision's under-seat bay is generous for its class and swallows a helmet, a poncho and a small bag together; the Click's bay is smaller and often holds a helmet or a bag, not both. If you commute with a laptop, carry groceries or stow gear between stops, that difference shapes your daily routine: one machine carries your life, the other carries a subset of it.

Passengers tilt the same way. Both scooters are light, but the Vision's seat and suspension cope with a second rider more comfortably, while the Click feels its load sooner, especially over bumps and on take-off. Riding two-up is common on both in Hanoi, and short two-up hops are fine on either, but a rider who carries a colleague or a partner regularly should give the Vision the nod and confirm two-up guidance with the shop at handover.

Practicality day to day also includes the small things: where your phone goes, where your lock lives, how the machine handles a bag between your feet on the flat footboard, and how easily it pushes into the last gap in a parking row. Both score well, and the Vision's extra room makes it fractionally more forgiving, while the Click's compactness makes it fractionally easier to squeeze into tight sidewalk rows near markets and in the Old Quarter.

The sit test and the route test

Numbers end the debate about price; only your body ends the debate about comfort. Visit the shop at 112 Nguyen Van Cu during opening hours, sit on both machines, and compare seat height against your inseam, lever reach against your hands, and the mirror view from your natural posture. Five minutes on each scooter answers questions that a month of reading cannot, and the staff are used to exactly this kind of comparison visit.

Then test with your real load. Hang your daily bag on the handlebar or between your feet, stow your helmet under the seat, sit a passenger if you usually ride with one, and feel how each machine settles. If you can, think through your actual route and where it goes: the potholed stretch, the bridge, the narrow lane near your office. The scooter that feels right loaded, on a route like yours, is the one to rent.

Use the visit for the paperwork questions too: which papers you carry when riding, how fuel is handled at handover and return, what the deposit arrangement covers and how monthly extensions work. Asking everything at once, while you stand between the two scooters, turns a vague comparison into a finished decision in a single visit.

How to decide and what to ask

Reduce the decision to four questions. How far do I ride on a normal day? What do I carry, and with whom? How many days a week do I ride? And what monthly figure works for my budget? Short, central, solo and cost-conscious points to the Click; longer, loaded, two-up and comfort-seeking points to the Vision. Most riders know their own answer within a minute of reading those four questions honestly.

Whatever you choose, protect the rental with routine: photograph the scooter at handover from all four sides, read the agreement once properly, note what it says about damage and the deposit, and keep the shop's contact details in your phone. The operator is open daily from 09:00 to 21:00, which fits around working hours, and a clean record from day one makes every extension and return a five-minute formality.

Finally, remember that both machines come from the same practical Honda lineage, both are easy and cheap to run, and neither will ruin a Hanoi stay. The Vision refines the experience and the Click economises on it. Pick the one that matches your week, agree the period that matches your rates, and either way you end up on one of the two best-suited small automatics for this city.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is cheaper to rent in Hanoi, the Honda Vision or the Honda Click?

The Click. It rents at 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, against the Vision's 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week and 1,800,000 to 2,000,000 VND per month at this operator.

Which model is better for daily commuting across Hanoi?

For longer daily distances, a full bag or a regular passenger, the Vision's comfort and storage justify its higher rate. For short, central, solo riding, the Click is the more economical and equally capable choice.

Does the Honda Vision have more storage than the Click?

Yes, noticeably. The Vision's under-seat bay holds a helmet, a poncho and a small bag together, while the Click's bay usually fits a helmet or a bag, not both. Commuters who carry daily loads prefer the Vision for this reason.

Can I ride two-up on either scooter?

Two-up riding is common on both in Hanoi. The Vision copes with a passenger more comfortably thanks to its seat and suspension; the Click feels its load sooner. Confirm two-up guidance and a second helmet with the shop at handover.

Should I rent by day, week or month?

If you ride three or more days a week, the weekly and especially monthly rates are far better value than daily payments for both models. Many riders take one week to test a model against their real route, then move to a monthly agreement.

Where can I compare the two models in person?

Visit Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu at 112 Nguyen Van Cu, Long Bien, Hanoi, open daily from 09:00 to 21:00. Sit on both, load your daily bag, and ask for the current monthly figures for each model before deciding.

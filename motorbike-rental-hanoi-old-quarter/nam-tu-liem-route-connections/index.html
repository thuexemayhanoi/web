Nam Tu Liem by Motorbike: Motorbike Route Connections in Hanoi

Nam Tu Liem sits at the far western end of the city's arterial layer, and its connection map is the longest-reading of any district in this range. The ride east runs the arterial line through Cau Giay and Ba Dinh's monument quarter to the Old Quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake, the ride north connects toward West Lake's open shores, the ride west begins almost at the doorstep, the suburbs and the open roads beyond, and the ride to the rental shop at Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu, 112 Nguyen Van Cu in Long Bien, crosses the city's middle and then the river on the bridges toward the Hoan Kiem side. Here are those connections, without made-up journey times, because Hanoi traffic is too unpredictable.

Nam Tu Liem's geography is a rider's sketch with three arrows and a long river leg. To the east, the main roads run through Cau Giay, then Ba Dinh's monument quarter, then into the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem Lake and the colonial streets, a long run to the centre but easy to follow the whole way. North runs the connection toward West Lake, Hanoi's largest, and the Tay Ho cafe belt around its shores. West runs the district's own edge, and the edge is thin, the suburbs and the long unhurried roads begin almost at the doorstep, the one connection the central districts cannot copy. And across the city's middle, then across the Red River, sits Long Bien, the shop's home district, the leg that bookends every rental.

The location is a trade-off: the longest trip to the centre and the longest river crossing of any district here, but also the city's widest boulevards for daily riding and an open western edge that turns a weekend into an out-of-town day without crossing anything first. The modern geometry makes every arrow legible, and the position makes each connection a deliberate ride rather than a daily accident.

The mistake is treating the district as remote and riding the eastern run daily while ignoring the rest. The western opening is Nam Tu Liem's own treasure, and the stay that rides all the arrows gets the postcard layer, the lake, the suburbs and the river, while the commute-only stay gets one long arterial. The connections are the plan, not the penalty.

The Eastern Arterial Run

The eastern run is the district's main artery ride and its longest. The boulevards run from Nam Tu Liem's wide lanes through Cau Giay and Ba Dinh's monument quarter, tree-lined and easy to follow, and past the monuments the route enters the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem Lake and the colonial streets around them. Travel time from Nam Tu Liem depends on traffic on the main roads, so ride in the steady window between rush hours, enjoy the trip and treat arriving as the reward.

The centre rewards the park-once habit more than any other destination. From Nam Tu Liem, ride east to the Old Quarter but stop at its edge: its one-way streets are quicker on foot and its parking is the most crowded in Hanoi. Use an attended row there and walk to the quarter and the lake. The cafe ring around Hoan Kiem Lake is the natural reward loop, and the ride home in the softer afternoon light, the long legible line westward, ends the day properly. A Nam Tu Liem rider who times this run well gets the centre on the best possible terms.

The mistake is riding the run at the commute's worst minute and blaming the district. The main road east carries the west side's commuter traffic at rush hour, so move your centre trip to the quieter window and the same route feels much easier. Time the run, park at the edge, and the centre delivers its best walk at the end of one of the city's best rides.

The River Crossing to Long Bien

The connection that bookends the rental is the longest, and it deserves the most deliberate planning. Long Bien and the Hoan Kiem side are joined by their bridges, the historic Long Bien Bridge and the wider Chuong Duong Bridge, and from Nam Tu Liem the trip to the shop at 112 Nguyen Van Cu crosses the city's middle before it crosses the river. No guide can give a reliable journey time for any route, so on pickup day choose the best time window, approach calmly, and treat the handover as the destination of your first ride rather than an errand before it. If you would rather not handle a bike on the first crossing, take a taxi to the shop, then ride back to Nam Tu Liem slowly and patiently.

The crossing stays a connection for the rental's whole life. Extensions, gear questions, service checks and the return all mean a trip to the shop, and each works better as one planned journey than as a rushed stop between two others. The support number saved at handover answers half the questions without the river at all, and the shop's advice on the approach is current in a way no published route can be. Cross like a border, unhurried and once per day at most, and batch the whole stay's river business into the fewest possible deliberate trips.

The mistake is zig-zag planning, treating the city's middle and the river as casual lines to be crossed whenever a thought occurs. From this district every crossing is the stay's biggest single journey, and the stay that scatters them spends its riding budget on the city's middle instead of the district's boulevards and the western open roads. Plan the river pocket like the event it is, and it becomes the stay's punctuation rather than its noise.

North Toward West Lake

The northern connection is the stay's reset ride. West Lake sits north of the arterial layer, and the ride from Nam Tu Liem toward the lake's shores passes Cau Giay's greener quarters and opens onto the lake's wide shoreline roads, out one shore and home the other, with temple gates and lakeside pull-ups stitching the way. The Tay Ho cafe belt on the western shore adds the linger layer, gentler to park in and easy to spend an afternoon in, and the loop's shape, always returning toward familiar territory, makes it the easiest big ride in the city to repeat.

The lake trades every district's pressure for open shores and steady boulevards, and for a Nam Tu Liem rider the loop is the confidence builder, the wide open roads that make the machine's habits second nature before the eastern run and the centre's contested edge rows ask for them. Morning coffee on the shore, an evening lap before dinner, many riders end up riding the loop several times a week, and from the district's western end the northern connection is worth every block of it.

The mistake is saving the north for a spare Sunday that never comes. Ride the lake loop regularly rather than once; start early in your stay and it becomes a calm weekly ride you won't want to miss. Ride the connection in the first week, then decide how often it belongs in the routine, the answer is usually several times.

The Western Opening at the Doorstep

The western connection is the district's signature. Nam Tu Liem already is the city's western edge, and its boulevards run outward into the suburbs almost immediately, past them the roads open into the long, unhurried riding that out-of-town days want, wide arterials, agricultural edges and the quiet roads that Hanoi's weekend riders collect. For long stays, this is Nam Tu Liem's biggest plus: longer rides start at the end of your street, and coming back through the district's simple road layout is an easy way to finish.

The western opening rewards two habits. First, plan realistically: distances here are long, so the time you leave matters more than the route. Ask the shop what the road past the suburbs is like right now. Second, plan the return. Long rides leave you tired, so finish on the district's wide boulevards and a watched row near home, not in the crowded centre parking. The west is the long-ride rider's treasure, treat the treasure and the return with equal respect.

The mistake is believing the city ends at its suburbs, or worse, that the district's edge is a limit rather than a beginning. The western connection is where the ride becomes the point, and a stay based in Nam Tu Liem that never once rides west has missed the district's own argument. Pick a quiet day, ask the shop for a current rough route, and ride out until the road feels comfortable.

Grouping Trips Across the Map

Connections work best when they are ridden deliberately, and the deliberate habit is grouping. Sort the stay's destinations into pockets: the home pocket, Nam Tu Liem's malls, the stadium quarter and the residential lanes, no connection required. The east pocket, Cau Giay, Ba Dinh, the Old Quarter and the lake around Hoan Kiem, one long arterial run and one edge park. The north pocket, West Lake and Tay Ho, the reset ride. The west pocket, the suburbs and the long rides, the doorstep project. And the river pocket, Long Bien and the shop, the deliberate leg, crossed for pickup, extensions and support. Five pockets, each rideable as one calm trip, and none that requires crossing another to reach it.

The grouping has a daily rhythm. The morning suits the farther pocket while the streets are fresh, the east pocket's long run belongs there, the afternoon suits the near one, and the evening belongs to the home district's mall rows and wide lanes. The west pocket is the weekend's, the north pocket the whenever, and the river pocket gets the day's best window when it must be ridden. A stay sorted into pockets rides each world calmly instead of zig-zagging daily, and the same destinations cost half the riding.

The mistake is the scatter plan, one far stop in each half of every day, the machine re-routed at every leg. From Nam Tu Liem, scattered trips cost more than from any other district here, since every area starts with a real ride, and a stay that scatters pays that cost four times a day. Write the pocket list, ride one pocket per day, and the map becomes the plan's budget, spent deliberately.

Matching the Machine to the Menu

The connection menu is a real input to the machine choice, and the verified fleet covers it in two tiers. The entry tier, Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Yamaha Mio and Honda Click at 150,000 VND per day, 700,000 VND per week and 900,000 to 1,200,000 VND per month, suits the city pockets of the menu, light, simple machines that hold the boulevards' pace and slot into the centre's tight edge rows. A solo rider on a city week needs nothing more, and the light machine earns its keep on the centre days.

The step-up tier serves the menu's long end. The Honda Vision is the comfortable pick when you ride the lake with a passenger or head west with bags: it has more storage and rents for 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week or 1,800,000-2,000,000 VND per month. The Honda Air Blade and the electric motorbike, 200,000 VND per day, 1,000,000 VND per week and 1,500,000 VND per month, suit the arterial tempo and the clean, quiet city loops respectively, and the Air Blade in particular matches the multi-stream character of the district's wide boulevards and the western arterials. All prices are the shop’s published rates, with monthly prices quoted at the shop as a range.

The mistake is choosing the machine before the plan. A week of city rides suits an entry-level bike, while a month of western trips with a passenger suits a step-up model; settle the choice with the shop once you know your plans. Sketch the connections first, then let the machine follow, and every pocket rides on the right one.

A Week Built on the Connections

A Nam Tu Liem week sequences itself around the map. Day one is pickup: get to the shop, collect the bike, ride carefully back through the city centre, and spend a relaxed evening near your Nam Tu Liem base. Day two stays home, the malls, the stadium quarter and the residential lanes at an amble. Day three rides the eastern arterial through Cau Giay and Ba Dinh to the Old Quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake, parked at the edge and walked. Day four rides north toward the lake loop and the Tay Ho cafe belt, and day five, or the first weekend, rides west into the suburbs for the long unhurried version of the district. From day six, choose rides by weather and preference, and combine any shop errands into one planned river crossing.

The rental period follows your plan. For two or three days of short local rides, the daily rate works: 150,000 VND entry, 200,000 VND step-up. If you plan to use all the routes over a week, by about day five the weekly price of 700,000 VND entry or 1,000,000 VND step-up wins. If Nam Tu Liem is your base for a month, with city rides on weekdays and trips west at weekends, the monthly rates make sense: 900,000-1,200,000 VND for entry bikes and 1,500,000-2,000,000 VND for step-up, quoted by the shop as ranges.

The mistake is a stay that treats the connections as complications instead of the plan itself. A Nam Tu Liem rental's whole value is position, the centre one long arterial east, the lake one connection north, the long roads one stretch west at the doorstep, the shop one deliberate crossing beyond the middle. At handover, ask the team at Motorbike Rental - Nguyen Tu to go through your route plan, match the bike and rental period to your week, and inspect the bike with you. Ride the pockets in order, and Nam Tu Liem delivers the whole city, and the roads beyond it, from one calm base.

Frequently asked questions about Nam Tu Liem route connections

How do I ride from Nam Tu Liem to the Old Quarter by motorbike?

Ride the eastern main roads through Cau Giay and Ba Dinh's monument quarter to the centre, a long but easy-to-follow boulevard route where traffic flow matters more than distance. Park once in an attended row at the Old Quarter's edge and walk the quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake, its one-way weave is slower on a machine than on foot and its parking is the city's most contested. Time the run for the steady period between commute peaks, since no guide can give reliable journey times.

Is West Lake reachable from Nam Tu Liem by motorbike?

Yes, the northern connection runs from the district through Cau Giay's greener quarters toward the lake's shores, and from there the shoreline road carries the loop into the Tay Ho cafe belt. The loop is also good open-road practice, with wide boulevards and landscaped shores that always lead back to familiar streets, and many riders ride it every week. It is the stay's reset ride and the best confidence builder on the menu.

How do I reach the rental shop in Long Bien from Nam Tu Liem?

The shop at 112 Nguyen Van Cu is on the far side of the Red River, so from Nam Tu Liem you ride through central Hanoi first, then cross on Long Bien Bridge, the old one, or the broader Chuong Duong Bridge. Journey times vary, so plan pickup and every later shop trip carefully, one crossing per day at most, with extensions, questions and the return grouped into the same calm journey, or take a taxi to the shop and ride the bike home.
